## The full new Terms text

Version 2026-11-13. Key points above the box (unchanged):

- X2F never holds your coins.
- If you post an ad, you pay a 1% fee when the trade completes.
- Moderators can read your trade chat only if a dispute is opened.
- Completed trades are erased on day 30.
- Disputed trades are kept up to 90 days, cleaned by our privacy tool, to train our moderators.
- No IP addresses are logged.
- We answer lawful warrants with the little we have.
- X2F is built to last, for people who trade regularly, so an account not signed into for 60 days is closed.

### 1. What X2F is

X2F is a place where like-minded, privacy-focused people and groups come together to trade Monero (XMR) with each other. Sellers exchange their hard-earned XMR for the payment method that suits them, and buyers pay for XMR in the way that is easiest for them, with cash, bank transfers or payment apps. X2F is a facilitator. It brings traders together and lets them trade safely and securely, with a built-in escrow system and moderators to resolve disputes if one ever happens. It is not a party to your trades, it is not a bank or an exchange, and it never holds your coins.

### 2. Your account

You need an account to trade or post an ad. X2F is completely privacy-focused, so you are never asked for an email address or a phone number.

You pick a private username, which you type to log in and only you see, and a public display name, which is how other traders see you. Keep your login details and your recovery code safe and private. X2F has no way to reset them for you, so if you lose them you may lose the account with no way to recover it. That is why we give you ample, highly secure ways to protect your account. Your coins stay safe in your own wallet either way, but your account age and reputation are irreplaceable.

One person, one account, with one exception: if you want others to help you trade, you can set them up as team members of your account, each with their own login. Do not sell, share or lend your account or your login.

### 3. Trades and escrow

Your coins stay in your own wallet, except while they are locked in a trade’s 2-of-3 escrow. X2F holds one of the three escrow keys and can never move coins on its own. Escrow payments are ordinary Monero transactions, so amounts and addresses stay hidden on the blockchain.

You decide whom you trade with and on what terms. Read the vendor’s terms before you take an offer. Buyer and seller beware: learning how everything works helps keep you from being put at a disadvantage or taken advantage of. Whatever happens, our moderators are here to see that fair endings are happy endings.

Only the seller funds the escrow, and funding it is a commitment to the trade. The buyer pays exactly as the trade says, with the payment method that was requested, posts proof of payment and marks the trade paid once the payment is complete. The seller confirms that the payment has arrived through that payment method and releases the XMR, which goes straight to the buyer’s own wallet. A release cannot be instantly undone, but it can be disputed, and we will recover the funds for the wronged party to the best of our ability. Hopefully the moderator settles it amicably and it ends as a normal trade.

A screenshot counts as proof, but it is not a guarantee: anything can be edited these days. Always check that the payment has arrived in your own account before you release the escrow.

Each trade has timers, and they are based on the payment method chosen. For example, if a payment method is usually instant but can take up to 2 hours, the buyer gets 2 hours to mark the trade paid using the payment method requested. If that time runs out and the buyer has not marked the trade paid, the seller can cancel the trade, and the escrowed XMR starts returning to the seller’s wallet at once. If the time has nearly run out:

- Send the payment as requested and mark the trade paid before the time runs out. Marking it paid in time stops the seller from cancelling once the time is up.
- If you may be late, tell the other trader, so they are not waiting to cancel at the first chance they get.
- Stay in contact and answer when the other trader reaches out. If you are the one not getting an answer, send a “nudge”, an audio alert that grabs their attention.
- If you paid late and the seller cancels, open a dispute and we will help you get your money back. The trade screen always shows the time left.

### 4. Fees

X2F collects a fee of 1% of the total amount traded, paid in XMR. Whoever posts the ad pays the 1% fee, automatically deducted when the trade completes. If you post an ad to sell XMR, you pay 1% of the XMR you sell. If you post an ad to buy XMR, you pay 1% of the XMR you buy. Very simple. We don’t get paid unless you both do. Responding to someone else’s ad is always free. If a moderator fully reverses a trade, there is no fee.

Monero network fees are paid as the trade screen shows. X2F does not set them.

### 5. Disputes and moderators

If something doesn’t go as planned, or you and the other trader disagree and don’t manage to sort it out yourselves, either of you can open a dispute, both during the trade and for a short time after it completes. The trade screen shows how long you have. A moderator then reads the trade and its chat, asks for evidence and decides the outcome.

Chats are encrypted. If a dispute is opened, the moderator assigned to it can decrypt the trade’s chat and has full access to the whole conversation and the record of what happened in the trade. If the decision is appealed, a senior moderator or X2F’s owner can see the same. We log every action our moderators take, so we can audit our own staff and make sure of the best outcome for everyone. A moderator’s goal is ultimately to see a trade through to completion. If that is not possible, the next best outcome is a full reversal, the same as though the trade never happened. If either party is not satisfied with the outcome of the dispute, they can request an appeal within the time shown with the decision, and a senior moderator who had no part in the first decision makes the final, binding decision.

An appeal can be requested only once per dispute and, as mentioned, decisions are final and binding. Our moderators are fair, and we are confident we will find the outcome that is best for everyone.

### 6. Privacy and data

A completed trade, chat included, is available for 30 days, after which the entire trade is purged. The only exception is a disputed trade: it is kept for 90 days after it completes, scrubbed of personal and confidential information by our internal privacy tool and used to train our moderators. At the 90-day mark the whole disputed trade is purged, no exceptions. X2F does not track you, does not use analytics and removes location and device details from uploads.

No IP addresses are logged, even in disputes.

### 7. Police and government requests

If police or a government agency sends us a lawful request for data backed by a warrant, we will provide what little information we hold. That is exactly why we don't log IP addresses or ask for your name, phone number or email address: we can't hand over what we never collect. [Lawyer to word this.]

### 8. Prohibited use

Do not use X2F for money from crime, to hide where money came from, to break the law where you live, or to violate anti-money-laundering or counter-terrorist-financing (AML/CFT) laws. That’s not what the site was designed for.

Do not cheat, threaten or mislead other traders. Never ask anyone to trade or chat outside X2F; doing so loses you all the protections the site offers.

Do not try to break, overload or get around X2F’s security.

We may pause or close any account at our own discretion, without warning and without always giving a reason. If we feel an account is a threat to the site or to our users, we will take action to protect them and ourselves. We do not take the security of our users lightly. If you think we got it wrong, you can reach out to a moderator. Coins in escrow are then settled by a moderator under the dispute rules.

### 9. Liability, changes and law

X2F is provided as it is. Trading carries risk, including the risk that the other trader acts in bad faith and that the price of Monero changes. Take care, and only trade what you can afford to lose.

Remember, this is a new platform, so we are evolving based on feedback. No rule is permanent, and every rule can be reconsidered and changed for the betterment of the site and our users. We strongly encourage you to leave feedback about the site and help shape this platform. When these Terms change, X2F shows them again at your next login, and trading waits until you accept the new version.

## Owner questions

- **OQ-1 (who can cancel when time runs out).** He wrote: "whoever started the trade can immediately cancel and receive the funds from escrow deposited back into their account". In X2F only the seller funds the escrow, so only the seller gets coins back, and by ruling only the seller can cancel once the buyer's time is up. The draft says "the seller can cancel the trade, and the escrowed XMR starts returning to the seller's wallet at once". Is that what he meant? The person who started the trade may be the buyer, who took a sell ad. **Recommend:** keep "the seller".
- **OQ-2 ("You are a trader or a vendor.").** The meaning is unclear, so it is left out. Did he mean a definition of who is who (someone who responds to ads is a "trader" and someone who posts them is a "vendor")? Or did he mean that everyone is one or the other? If it is a definition, it could go in section 4 as: "Whoever posts an ad is the vendor; whoever responds to it is the trader." **Recommend:** ask him. The Terms already use "vendor" once (3 p2, "the vendor's terms"), so a definition would help.
