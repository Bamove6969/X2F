## Verdict

**APPROVED WITH MINOR for landing dark; DO NOT LIGHT in production until M1 is fixed and the interop run below has passed.**

The cryptography is right. Every byte of the libsignal framing, the X3DH ordering, the HKDF salts and infos, the chain and message-key derivations, the MAC construction and the OMEMO 0.3 envelope match libsignal and the XEP; the known-answer vectors are genuinely libsignal's; the hand-written Edwards arithmetic is correct and touches a secret exactly once in the deployment's lifetime. Nothing sends while `X2F_XMPP_*` is unset (checked, section 9). What remains is a stream-parsing weakness inherited from the parent branch that the new OMEMO step makes more interesting (M1), one availability property of the stateless design the owner should hear (M2), and small hardening items. No finding puts a user's secret at risk: the notice text is a fixed sentence, X2F never receives, and the only real secret in the whole path is the six-digit set-up code, whose only use is by the person who typed the address.

## Findings

Severity: High = a secret or an integrity property is lost; Medium = an attacker gets an effect they should not; Low = hardening; Info = for the record.

### M1 (Medium): after SASL, the sender accepts stanzas by id only, with constant ids, and treats `<failure` anywhere in the buffer as a fatal error; anyone who knows X2F's full JID can inject

- Where: `apps/api/src/jabber/xmpp.ts:86` (`Reader.until`: `/<stream:error[\s>]|<failure[\s>]/` tested on the whole buffer at every step), `xmpp.ts:115-116` (`Reader.iq` matches `<iq … id='X'>` with no check of `from`), `xmpp.ts:211-221` (ids `omemo-pub-dl`, `omemo-pub-b`, `omemo-dl`, `omemo-b0`…, constant), `xmpp.ts:137, 241-243` (`bounceOf(id)`: a `<message type='error' id=…>` from anyone), `xmpp.ts:204` (resource `x2f-alerts`, constant, so the full JID `x2f-alerts@domain/x2f-alerts` is known to every recipient: it is the `from` of every notice they get).
- The prior review (L2 b) said third-party stanzas cannot arrive because the client sends no presence. That is wrong for stanzas addressed to the full JID: RFC 6121 §8.5.3.1 makes a server deliver a stanza addressed to an exactly matching connected resource regardless of presence. Legitimate replies (the clients' key-transport "session completed" messages) go to the bare JID and are stored offline, as the plan says; deliberate ones need not.
- What a hostile recipient can do: send `<message to='x2f-alerts@d/x2f-alerts'><failure xmlns='x'/></message>` or a forged `<message type='error' id='x2f…'>` (they know the id: it is on the notice they received) during the ping window: the send fails, the outbox retries up to `OUTBOX_ATTEMPTS`, each retry a new session and a duplicate notice, then the notice is dropped. Self-harm plus log noise. They can also publish a bundle whose item contains a `<failure xmlns='x'/>` element (PEP payload is arbitrary XML, and the PEP answer lands in the same buffer): every notice to them fails. Also self-harm.
- What a third party who knows the full JID can do (any X2F Jabber user, or anyone they tell): flood the full JID with stanzas carrying `<failure xmlns='x'/>` so that every send fails while the flood lasts, bounded only by the server's rate limit and our 64 KB buffer cap (which also fails the send). Or flood `<iq type='result' id='omemo-dl'>` with their own device list and `<iq type='result' id='omemo-b0'>` with their own bundle: if their answers arrive before the recipient's server's, the sender encrypts the notice, or a set-up test code, to the attacker's device. The test code is only useful to the person who typed the address (the victim's own session), and the notice text is fixed, so the confidentiality loss is small, but the sender has still been talked into encrypting to the wrong key, and RFC 6120 §8.1.2.1 says a client must check that an IQ result's `from` matches the request's `to`.
- Fix (engineering, no behaviour change): random IQ and message ids (`randomBytes(8).toString('hex')`); in `Reader.iq` accept an answer only when its `from` is the request's `to`, or absent / our own bare JID / our server's domain for requests to our own account; in `bounceOf` require `from` to be the recipient's bare JID or their domain (the server stamps `from`; s2s authenticates the domain); and test `<failure` and `<stream:error` only where they can legitimately appear: `<failure>` during SASL, `<stream:error>` only as a top-level element (match `^` of the buffer after consuming complete stanzas, or stop checking `<failure` after `<success>`). Add tests with a fake server that injects a stranger's stanza between the request and the real answer.

### M2 (Medium, availability of the owner's mandated channel): a stale bundle makes a stateless notice undecryptable with a measurable probability when several notices reach an offline device

- Where: `packages/omemo/src/omemo.ts:51` (one prekey picked at random from the bundle as published now), `docs/jabber/omemo-plan.md` section 2.8 (one fresh X3DH and PreKeySignalMessage per notice).
- How libsignal receivers behave (`SessionBuilder.processV3`): a PreKeySignalMessage names a one-time prekey id; after a successful decrypt the client deletes that prekey (`preKeyStore.removePreKey`) and republishes a bundle without it (Conversations tops the list back up to 100 on every prekey message). A later message naming a deleted prekey fails with `InvalidKeyIdException`: the client shows "message could not be decrypted" and nothing recovers it.
- The sender fetches the bundle at every send, so while the client is online and republishes promptly, the only exposure is the few seconds between its consumption and its republish. While the client is offline, every notice is encrypted against the same 100 published prekeys and each picks one at random: for k notices waiting, the chance that two picked the same prekey is about 1 − exp(−k(k−1)/200): k=2: 1%, k=3: 3%, k=5: 10%, k=10: 36%, k=15: 65%. Two notices for one change are common (a team change tells the team account; the previous review's M2 flood scenario makes k large). The test code message is the first and usually only message, so set-up is not affected.
- This is inherent in "no session state" (the plan says so: owner question 3) and the plan's decision is reasonable; the owner should know the number. Mitigations, in order of cost: (a) coalesce unsent notices per (account, kind) in the outbox, which the previous review recommended anyway (M2) and which makes k small; (b) pick prekeys without replacement across the notices sent in one outbox tick to the same recipient (cheap, removes the within-tick case entirely); (c) a stateful sender later. Also a `trade-attention` and an `account-change` to the same offline device are two prekey picks; (b) covers them.
- Fix now: (b) plus the interop test "two notices while the phone is offline" below. Owner question 5 carries (a).

### L1 (Low): the hand-written Edwards/XEdDSA arithmetic is correct but not constant-time; accept, with a cheap way to remove it from the production path

- Where: `packages/omemo/src/edwards.ts:84-92` (`scalarMultBase`: double-and-add with a branch on the scalar bit), `:144-157` (`xeddsaSign`).
- Correctness, checked: the formulas are RFC 8032 §5.1.4's unified addition with 2d (complete for a = −1, so doubling by `add(q, q)` is valid), the base point is recovered with sign 0 (its x is even), constants are reduced (`D = mod(-121665 · 121666^-1)`), `recoverX` follows RFC 8032 §5.1.3 including the `x = 0, sign = 1` refusal, scalars are reduced mod L with the 512-bit hash reduced correctly, and the signing matches libsignal's `crypto_sign_modified` line for line: r = SHA-512(0xFE ‖ 0xFF×31 ‖ a ‖ M ‖ Z) mod L, R = rB, h = SHA-512(R ‖ A ‖ M) mod L, S = r + h·a, A's sign bit in S[63]. The one difference from libsignal is that `xeddsaSign` clamps the private key before use while `curve25519_sign` uses it as given; libsignal's keys are clamped at generation (as are OpenSSL's, which store the clamped scalar), so the results are identical for every key this code will ever hold, and the clamp makes a wrongly stored key still sign for the X25519 public key OpenSSL derives from it.
- The KATs are libsignal's. I fetched `tests/src/test/java/org/whispersystems/libsignal/ecc/Curve25519Test.java` from signalapp/libsignal-protocol-java and extracted its byte arrays: `alicePublic/alicePrivate/bobPublic/bobPrivate/shared` in `x25519.test.ts:6-10` and `aliceIdentityPrivate/aliceIdentityPublic/aliceEphemeralPublic/aliceSignature` in `edwards.test.ts:10-15` match exactly. libsignal publishes no deterministic signing vector (the nonce is random), so signing is checked by the verifier, by the differential test against OpenSSL (`edwards.test.ts:45-54`, 200 seeds) and by my cross-check above.
- Sign-bit convention: libsignal keeps A's sign bit as computed and carries it in S's top bit; the verifier moves it into the Edwards key derived from u. The code does that (`edwards.ts:155, 162-165`); half of random keys have the bit set and all verified. Decompression: `xeddsaVerify` refuses u ≥ p and u = p−1 (libsignal reduces mod p and maps u = −1 to y = 0 instead); OpenSSL's verifier additionally insists on S < L. All three are stricter than libsignal only on inputs no real client produces (an X25519 public key is always canonical and < p; a real signature has S < L), so there is no interop effect, and stricter is right for a verifier of untrusted bundles. Small-order u (0, 1, the order-8 points) verify false; see L2 for what X25519 does with them.
- Timing. What secret the bigint code sees: our long-lived identity private scalar a and the per-signature nonce r, in `generateBundleSecrets` (`bundle.ts:32`), which runs exactly once: `loadOmemoBundle` (`apps/api/src/jabber/omemo.ts:194-196`) generates the bundle when `bundle_sealed` is null and never again; the signed prekey is never rotated. Nothing per notice touches the bigint code with a secret: `encryptMessage` calls `xeddsaVerify` (public inputs only) and OpenSSL for X25519, HKDF, HMAC, AES; `parseBundleSecrets` verifies (public) and derives public keys through OpenSSL. So the side channel is one ~2.5 ms execution at first use, on a server an attacker reaches only through Tor, whose moment the attacker neither triggers nor observes. A remote timing attack needs many measurements of secret-dependent durations; here there is one, and it is not observable. The weight dependence I measured (2.1 to 3.7 ms across extreme scalars, and leaking r from one signature recovers a) only matters to a co-resident attacker with a cache or branch-timing probe on the API host during that millisecond, who could read the key from memory anyway. **Recommendation: accept** for this deployment, as the plan does.
- Mitigate/replace, if the owner or lead wants no secret in hand-written arithmetic at all (about 30 lines, no new dependency): generate X2F's identity as an Ed25519 key pair (`generateKeyPairSync('ed25519')`); the X25519 private scalar is a = clamp(SHA-512(seed)[0..32]) and the Montgomery identity key is u = (1 + y)/(1 − y) of the Ed25519 public key (`montgomeryFromEdwards`, public data); sign the signed prekey with node's Ed25519 `sign(null, msg, seedKey)` and OR A's sign bit into byte 63. That is a valid XEdDSA signature (R = rB with a deterministic r, S = r + H(R‖A‖M)·a over the same a and A; libsignal's `curve25519_verify` clears the bit and checks the Ed25519 equation), and the test file already does exactly this in `xedKey()` (`apps/api/test/xmpp.test.ts:60-74`) and verifies it with the production verifier. OpenSSL then does every secret operation in constant time, `edwards.ts` keeps `montgomeryFromEdwards` and the verifier, and `scalarMultBase`/`xeddsaSign` become test-only. The identity row is created on first use and production is not set up, so nothing needs migrating. Downside: none for this sender (deterministic nonces are fine; XEdDSA's random Z only adds fault resistance).

### L2 (Low): one malformed device in a recipient's list fails the whole send instead of being skipped

- Where: `packages/omemo/src/omemo.ts:47-63` (`bundles.map` throws on the first bad bundle), `packages/omemo/src/x25519.ts:38-42`. OpenSSL refuses an X25519 agreement that comes out all-zero (`failed during derivation`, confirmed for u = 0, 1, p−1, p, p+1 and the order-8 points), so a device that publishes a small-order identity key, signed prekey or prekey makes `initAlice` throw and `encryptMessage` throws for every device; `xmpp.ts:228-230` turns that into `xmpp: omemo failed` and nothing is sent to the user's other devices. Only the user (or their server) can publish such a bundle, so the harm is to themselves, but a user with one broken client should still get the notice on their good one.
- Fix: catch per device, skip it, send when at least one device remains, as `parseBundle` already does for bundles that do not verify. Keep throwing when none remains.

### L3 (Low): zeroing the private keys after each use is cosmetic; do not rely on or advertise it

- Where: `apps/api/src/jabber/omemo.ts:226-230` (`done`: `fill(0)` on the identity and bundle private keys), `packages/omemo/src/x25519.ts:21` (`Buffer.concat([PKCS8_X25519, priv])` makes an unzeroed copy of every private key passed in, and OpenSSL's key objects hold another), `omemo.ts:166-182` (the ephemeral base key, ratchet key, root and chain keys, message keys and the 16-byte payload key are never zeroed). None of this is a vulnerability in a JS process (a reader of the heap has everything anyway), and loading per use so that a rotated ring is picked up is good. Just keep the comment honest ("not kept in memory between sends" is true of the references, not of the bytes).

### L4 (Low): `announce()` errors are not wrapped like `encrypt()` errors; `JSON.parse` of the decrypted bundle can quote plaintext bytes in its message

- Where: `xmpp.ts:210` (`await opts.announce()` inside `send`, not caught, so `loadOmemoIdentity`/`loadOmemoBundle`/`parseBundleSecrets` messages propagate out of `send`), `packages/omemo/src/bundle.ts:64` (V8's `JSON.parse` error includes a snippet of the source, here the *decrypted* bundle JSON, which holds private keys in hex). Today nothing logs `e.message` from `send` (`outbox.ts` logs counts only, `routes.ts:156` logs `test-not-sent` only), and the blob is AES-GCM-authenticated so malformed JSON means a bug, not an attacker. Fix: wrap `announce()` like `encrypt()` (`throw new XmppError('omemo')`), and in `parseBundleSecrets` catch the parse error and rethrow a fixed message.

### L5 (Low, interop): no XEP-0380 `<encryption>` hint and no fallback `<body>`

- Where: `xmpp.ts:235-237`. Every OMEMO 0.3 client decrypts a message that has only `<encrypted>` (Conversations, Dino, Gajim, Monal all key on the element), so this is not a blocker. XEP-0384 0.3.0 §4.6 says the sender SHOULD add `<encryption xmlns='urn:xmpp:eme:0' namespace='eu.siacs.conversations.axolotl' name='OMEMO'/>` (an element, no text; it only tells a client without OMEMO what it is looking at) and a fallback `<body>`. The body is a behaviour decision (owner question 2); EME is engineering and I recommend adding it.

### L6 (Info): where the branch is stricter or different from libsignal, and why it does not matter

- `xeddsaVerify` refuses u ≥ p, u = p−1, S ≥ L (L1): no real input is affected.
- `parseBundle` requires at least one prekey (`apps/api/src/jabber/omemo.ts:129`) where libsignal would start a session without one; XEP-0384 0.3.0 §4.3 requires 20 to 100 in a bundle, so a prekey-less bundle is a broken client, and refusing is right (the fallback is still implemented and tested in `ratchet.test.ts:63-71`).
- The registration id is `1 + deviceId % 16380` (`omemo.ts:32`), in libsignal's range; receivers store and ignore it.
- `signedPreKeyId` is random in 1..0xFFFFFE and prekey ids are 1..100: both within libsignal's `Medium.MAX_VALUE`; the prekey id 0xFFFFFF, which libsignal treats as "no prekey", is never used.
- The encoder writes the PreKeySignalMessage fields as 5,1,6,2,3,4 (`message.ts:105-112`); protobuf parsers accept any order. `counter = 0` is written explicitly (proto2 `optional`), which libsignal's `hasCounter()` check needs.
- `prekey='true'` on `<key>`: Conversations `Boolean.parseBoolean`, Dino `get_attribute_bool`, Gajim and Monal accept `true`.
- The IV is 12 bytes. XEP-0384 0.3.0 does not fix the IV length (it says only "fresh, randomly generated key/IV pairs", 16-byte key, tag ≥ 128 bits). Conversations sends 12 and accepts 12 or 16; the plan says the others accept 12. The interop run must confirm it for Gajim and Monal (both had 16-only decryptors in old versions).
- PEP `pubsub#access_model=open` is not in XEP-0384 0.3.0 at all (it arrived in 0.4); it is what Conversations and the others do so that other servers' users can fetch the bundle, and it is right.

### L7 (Info): what the tests prove and what only interop can

- Primitives have external vectors (libsignal agreement and signature, RFC 5869, RFC 4231) and the differential test against OpenSSL. The protobuf bytes are hand-checked. The SignalMessage bytes and MAC are checked byte for byte against an independent HMAC in the test.
- X3DH and the Double Ratchet have no published vectors from Signal; `ratchet.test.ts` checks both halves written by the same author against each other plus the libsignal-specific observable facts (Alice's first ratchet key is fresh, Bob ratchets to a fresh key on her first message, `previousCounter`, skipped keys used once, the 2000 cap, the 5-chain cap, state untouched on failure, 40 seeded random schedules). I checked the derivations by reading them against libsignal's `RatchetingSession`, `RootKey`, `ChainKey`, `SessionCipher.getOrCreateChainKey/getOrCreateMessageKeys` and found no difference (the X3DH DH order IK_A·SPK_B, EK_A·IK_B, EK_A·SPK_B, [EK_A·OPK_B] after 32 × 0xFF; `WhisperText` with a 32-zero-byte salt; `WhisperRatchet` with the root key as salt; `0x01`/`0x02` seeds; `WhisperMessageKeys` 80 bytes split 32/32/16; AES-256-CBC/PKCS#7; HMAC-SHA-256 over sender IK ‖ receiver IK ‖ version ‖ protobuf, truncated to 8). The receiving side in `OmemoDevice` is also what Conversations does on receipt (prekey consumed, session kept by sender identity and sid). A real client is still the only proof that a real `SessionCipher` accepts the bytes.

### L8 (Info): consequences of PEP with open access and blind trust (question 6)

- The recipient's server (or any server on the s2s path, or X2F's own server) can replace the recipient's device list and bundles with keys it holds. Under blind trust the sender encrypts to them: the server then reads the notice. What it learns: that this JID is an X2F user and the moment of each account change or dispute (it already learns both from the stanza's existence, encrypted or not), and the six-digit test code, which is useful only to the person holding the X2F session that typed the address. Nothing in the notice says what changed. It can also drop devices from the list (the notice reaches fewer devices) or add its own (it gets a copy). Pinning the first-seen identity key per address would stop the swap for returning users at the cost the plan names (a new phone goes silent); given what the text contains, blind trust is the right call (owner question 1).
- X2F's own server can swap X2F's bundle. Recipients then encrypt their replies to the server's key; X2F never reads replies, so nothing is lost, and X2F's notices are still signed under X2F's real identity key, which clients see as a second device of the same sender. It can delete our nodes; the sender republishes on every connection. It can refuse `access_model=open` or publish-options (`precondition-not-met` if the node exists with another configuration): every send then fails with `xmpp: publish failed`, visible in the outbox counts; that is a set-up problem for the interop run, not a security one.
- X2F's server also sees the full list of X2F Jabber users and the timing of every change (previous review M1, unchanged: it is why p11-42, X2F's own onion server, matters).

### L9 (Info): the stateless design against replay, reordering and prekey exhaustion (question 7)

- Replay of a captured notice to the client: libsignal sees a PreKeySignalMessage with a base key it already has a session for (`hasSessionState`), reuses that session, finds counter 0 already used and throws `DuplicateMessageException`; the client drops it silently. No duplicate is shown, no prekey is consumed. Checked in `ratchet.test.ts:83-84` and by reading `SessionBuilder.processV3`.
- Reordering: every notice is its own session at counter 0, so order is irrelevant; libsignal archives the previous session on each new prekey message and keeps 40, which is more than any outbox burst.
- Prekey exhaustion of X2F: impossible, X2F never receives and never consumes its own prekeys; the 100 exist so the bundle is well-formed. Prekey exhaustion of the recipient by X2F: every notice burns one of the recipient's prekeys per device; a hostile counterparty can cause `trade-attention` notices only by opening disputes on trades with the victim, and a team member can cause `account-change` notices by cheap self-changes (previous review M2 a). Clients replenish, and an exhausted published list is still a list (the sender refuses only an empty one), so the effect is M2 above (undecryptable duplicates), not loss of the channel.
- The recipient's XMPP replies to our parser: see M1. The recipient's PEP payloads to our parser: `parseDeviceList` and `parseBundle` are regex-based and bounded (16 devices, 64 KB per answer, one XEdDSA verify and four X25519 per device): nothing can be made to hang or allocate much, and a crafted payload can only change what the recipient themselves receives.

### L10 (Info): the fake XMPP server against a real one (question 8)

What the fake assumes and a real server may do differently, all for the interop run: that PEP publish with publish-options (`persist_items`, `access_model=open`) is accepted on first publish (Prosody ≥ 0.11 and ejabberd do; older or misconfigured servers answer `feature-not-implemented` or `precondition-not-met`); that another server answers an anonymous PEP `items` request over s2s for a node with open access (it does only when the client published it open: Conversations, Dino, Gajim and Monal do); that nothing unsolicited arrives on the stream (real servers may send `<iq type='get'>` disco#info to our full JID from the recipient's client, roster pushes are not sent without interest, carbons are not enabled; a stray `<iq>` is harmless to the Reader today, M1 covers the hostile case); that the recipient's server does not block strangers (Prosody `mod_block_strangers`, ejabberd `mod_block_strangers`, and Conversations' own "block strangers" setting can drop the first message from a JID not in the roster; X2F sends no presence subscription); that offline storage and push wake the client for a message with `<encrypted>` and `<store/>` but no `<body>` (Prosody `mod_cloud_notify` and ejabberd `mod_push` both treat an `eu.siacs.conversations.axolotl` element as important); that TLS 1.2+ with a certificate for the JID's domain is offered on the direct-TLS port through Tor; and that the sender's fixed `x2f-alerts` resource is never already bound (RFC 6120 lets the server rename or conflict).

### L11 (Info, checked): nothing sends while `X2F_XMPP_*` is unset (question 9)

- `apps/api/src/config.ts:295-301`: `parseXmpp` returns null when `X2F_XMPP_JID` is unset or empty, and throws when any other `X2F_XMPP_*` is set without it; `config.ts:247` requires `X2F_JABBER_KEYS` when it is set.
- `apps/api/src/main.ts:92-94`: `jabberSender` exists only when `config.xmpp && config.jabberKeys`; `main.ts:109-111`: the outbox worker starts only with a sender; the routes answer `503 jabber-unavailable` without one (`jabber.test.ts:150-163`). No other site constructs `xmppSender` (grep). The `omemoEncryptor` is the only encryptor wired; `xmppSender` without one fails every send with `xmpp: omemo failed` (`xmpp.test.ts`, "without an encryptor"). The identity and bundle rows are created only on first use by the sender, so an unset deployment also writes nothing to `jabber_omemo_identity`.
- Tests: `xmpp.test.ts:560-565` (off by default), the config refusals at `:573-597`.

## The envelope (question 4), checked

`packages/omemo/src/envelope.ts:22-27`: AES-128-GCM with a 16-byte key, a 12-byte IV and a 16-byte tag, all three fresh per message from `randomBytes` evaluated at each call; the plaintext is the UTF-8 of `messageText(message)` and nothing else (`xmpp.ts:228`, `messageText` in `sender.ts:33-40`), one of three fixed sentences; `key ‖ tag` (32 bytes, `envelope.ts:36`) is what each device's session encrypts (`omemo.ts:45, 62`), which is Conversations' `PUT_AUTH_TAG_INTO_KEY` form and what current clients require. IV reuse is impossible because the key is fresh for every message and used for one encryption; even an RNG that repeated an IV would repeat it under a different key. The element is built from checked integers and base64 only (`envelope.ts:55-61`); `isEncryptedElement` (`apps/api/src/jabber/omemo.ts:134-137`) refuses anything else and any `<body>`.

## Key handling (question 5), checked

Identity private key sealed under `X2F_JABBER_KEYS` with context `x2f/jabber-omemo-identity/<public hex>`, bundle secrets under `x2f/jabber-omemo-bundle/<public hex>` (`apps/api/src/jabber/omemo.ts:150-151, 173, 189-201`); AES-256-GCM with the header and context as associated data (`auth/keystore.ts`), so a blob cannot be moved between contexts or key ids. `parseBundleSecrets` re-derives the public keys and refuses a bundle whose signature does not verify under the identity (`bundle.ts:74`), so a bundle moved to another identity row is refused (`omemo.test.ts:56`). Rewrap covers both blobs (`rewrap.ts:61-76`), tested (`jabber.test.ts:421-431`). Nothing in the jabber path logs anything but counts (`outbox.ts`, `routes.ts:156`); `xmpp.ts` errors are `xmpp: <step> failed`; the encryptor's errors are swallowed (`xmpp.ts:229-230`); L4 is the only gap. Our prekeys are never consumed (X2F never receives), so "never reused" is moot for us; the recipient's prekeys are picked at random per notice and each is used in one message (M2 is about the recipient deleting it before our next pick).

## Before the sender is lit in production: interop test plan

Run against a real server X2F controls (Prosody or ejabberd, reached through Tor, with PEP and publish-options), with X2F's account on it and each client on a second server (so the PEP fetch crosses s2s), from a build with M1 and L2 fixed:

1. **Publish**: first connection creates the device list and bundle nodes; `disco#items` on X2F's bare JID shows both; an anonymous fetch from the second server returns the bundle (open access). Repeat the connection: the publish is idempotent (`result`, same item id). Try once against a server configured without publish-options to see `xmpp: publish failed` and no message.
2. **Each client** (Conversations 2.17.x, Dino 0.4.x, Gajim 2.x with omemo-dr, Monal 6.x): a fresh account, X2F not in the roster. Send the set-up test code: the message appears, decrypted, from "X2F alerts", on every device of the account; note any client or server that drops it as a stranger (then the UI copy must tell users to add the address, owner question 3). Then one `account-change`: decrypted, no fallback body shown as garbage, no "unencrypted" warning.
3. **Two devices** on one account (phone and desktop): both decrypt the same notice; the device that was offline decrypts it when it comes back.
4. **Offline burst**: client offline, send 3 notices in one minute, bring it back: all 3 decrypt (with M2's fix (b) this must always hold within a tick; without it, repeat 20 times and record failures).
5. **Replenishment**: 101 notices over an hour to an online Conversations: every one decrypts and the published bundle keeps 100 prekeys.
6. **Replay**: replay a captured notice stanza from the server side: the client shows nothing new.
7. **Bounce**: a notice to a JID that does not exist on the second server fails the send (`xmpp: delivery failed`) within the bounce wait; a notice to a server that is down fails without a duplicate.
8. **IV and tag**: Gajim and Monal in particular decrypt the 12-byte IV and the 32-byte key‖tag (both had 16-byte-IV-only code in old versions).
9. **Hostile stanzas** (M1 regression): from a third account, flood X2F's full JID with `<failure xmlns='x'/>` messages and forged `<iq type='result' id=…>` during a send: the send still completes to the right recipient.
10. **Offline store**: after the run, count the key-transport messages waiting in X2F's offline store and set the cap or expiry the plan's owner question 5 asks for.

Record client versions, server versions and the outcome of each line in `docs/jabber/omemo-plan.md` before the flag is set in production.

## Owner questions (with recommendations)

1. **Trust (plan Q1)**: send to every device whose bundle verifies, with no fingerprint check (blind trust before verification)? Recommendation: yes. The notice text is fixed and reveals nothing but its timing; pinning would silently cut off a user with a new phone, and X2F has no way to show a fingerprint. A hostile server can read the fixed sentence either way (L8).
2. **Fallback `<body>`**: XEP-0384 0.3 recommends a plaintext fallback body such as "I sent you an OMEMO encrypted message but your client doesn't seem to support that" plus the EME hint. Recommendation: add the EME `<encryption>` element (engineering, no text), and keep **no `<body>`**, consistent with p11-31 and the previous review; a client without OMEMO shows nothing, which is the honest outcome.
3. **Roster**: X2F sends no presence subscription, so some servers and Conversations' "block strangers" setting can drop the first message. Recommendation: the set-up copy tells the user to add X2F's address as a contact before the test message (COPY-UNSIGNED until the owner signs), and the interop run (step 2) says whether it is needed per client.
4. **Licence (plan Q4)**: confirmed, no GPL or AGPL dependency was added; `packages/omemo` has no dependencies at all. Recommendation: note it in the rulings so nobody adds `@signalapp/libsignal-client` later.
5. **Stateless sessions (plan Q3) and M2**: accept one fresh prekey message per notice, with (a) the outbox coalescing unsent notices per (account, kind) as the previous review's M2 recommended and (b) no prekey reused within one outbox tick to the same recipient? Recommendation: yes to both; (b) is pure engineering, (a) changes how many messages a user sees for a burst of changes (the sentence never counts them, so "a message for every change" still holds).
6. **Signing implementation (L1)**: keep the hand-written XEdDSA signing (accepted risk, runs once) or replace it with OpenSSL's Ed25519 signer through a seed-derived identity? Recommendation: replace before lighting if the schedule allows (about 30 lines, already prototyped in the test's `xedKey()`); otherwise accept, as the plan does. This is an engineering choice, listed so the owner knows the trade.
7. **Offline store (plan Q5)**: every client answers a prekey message with a key-transport message to X2F's account, which X2F never reads and never fetches; on X2F's own server (p11-42) cap or expire that account's offline store. Recommendation: `mod_offline` limit of a few hundred messages or a 7-day expiry; not a code question.
8. **Kaidan and OMEMO 2 (plan Q2)**: a user whose only client is Kaidan gets "no OMEMO device found". Recommendation: accept now; the second encryptor can be added behind the same sender later.
