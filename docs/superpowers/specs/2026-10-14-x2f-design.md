# X2F design

Date: 2026-10-14
Status: draft for review

## 1. What X2F is

X2F is a peer-to-peer marketplace for trading Monero (XMR) for fiat money: cash, bank transfers and payment apps. It fills the gap LocalMonero left when it closed in 2024. It ships as one codebase that runs as a website and is wrapped as iOS and Android apps, so it feels native everywhere.

**Who it is for:** people who want to sell XMR for fiat (the off-ramp, our main focus) and people who want to buy XMR with fiat, without handing their coins or identity to a company.

**What success looks like**
- A first-time user completes a trade on a phone without help.
- No single breach of X2F's servers can move user funds.
- Disputes are answered within a published deadline.
- Traders return because their reputation is worth something.

**Principles** (every decision below is checked against these)
1. **Non-custodial.** X2F never holds enough keys to move a user's coins, and it holds **no wallet for users' coins**: no balances, no deposits, no in-app wallet. (X2F's own fee wallet receives only the 1% fee; see 6.6.) Coins stay in the user's own Monero wallet (Cake Wallet, Edge, Feather and so on) except while they are locked in a trade's 2-of-3 escrow. X2F is purely a facilitator.
2. **Privacy is a right.** No platform identity checks, no email or phone required, no tracking or analytics, metadata stripped from every upload, and data deleted after a set retention period.
3. **Security over speed.** Only mature, widely used, audited libraries; nothing experimental in production. External audit and penetration test before launch.
4. **Simple on the surface.** Multisig, Tor and key exchange happen behind the scenes. The user sees Fund, I've paid, Received.

Research behind this design: competitor report at https://claude.ai/artifact/3QFL5yy61UXqvvFFQ4jWRH (dead platforms, survivors, user complaints, escrow models).

## 2. The trade flow

### 2.1 Roles
- **Seller:** has XMR, wants fiat. Always the one who funds escrow.
- **Buyer:** has fiat, wants XMR.
- **Moderator:** an X2F staff member who holds the third escrow key and decides disputes.

Either side can post an ad: sellers post "selling XMR" ads, and buyers post "buying XMR" ads.

### 2.2 Step by step

1. **Browse.** Buy and sell boards show ads filtered by the payment methods and currency the user chooses, so they only see deals they can actually complete. Browsing needs no account.
2. **Chat.** A user may open free pre-trade chats with any number of counterparties. Chats cost nothing, carry no penalties and expire if ignored.
3. **Quote.** Picking an ad opens a quote screen. The seller enters an amount or taps Max. One screen then shows:
   - the XMR amount and the ad's rate,
   - the fiat total in both parties' currencies,
   - the network fee (always the fast priority),
   - no X2F fee line: the person taking an ad pays none. The ad poster's 1% is itemised on their released screen (see section 11, item 1),
   - one final total.

   Nothing changes after the user confirms.
4. **"Trade."** Whoever takes an offer taps **Trade** (a buyer taking a sell offer, or a seller taking a buy offer); the offer's poster is told "<username> accepted your offer". The buyer then picks **which of their own wallets gets paid**, from the wallets saved under Your wallets (see 6.4), or adds one there and then (an address from their wallet app; adding one asks the user to confirm it's them, see 6.4). The buyer's tap starts escrow setup between the apps and locks the buyer's deposit if this trade requires one (see 2.5). A buyer can have only one "I'm in" waiting for funding at a time per ad amount, so five sellers cannot fund five escrows for one buyer.
5. **Fund (the key turn).** The seller has **30 minutes** to fund from their own wallet. The main button opens their saved wallet app ("Open in Cake Wallet") with the escrow address and exact amount already filled in, so they only confirm; a QR code and a copyable address are the fallback. The window is longer than the 10 minutes first proposed because every seller now pays from an outside wallet. The price locks at this moment. **Offer pricing** (decided 2026-10-17): the poster picks either market price plus or minus a percentage (the default) or a fixed price per XMR, as Haveno does; the form warns that a fixed price goes stale when the market moves. **Funding is the commitment: once the escrow is funded, the seller cannot pull it back alone.** If the seller does not fund in time, the buyer is released with no penalty.
6. **Securing escrow.** Once the funding transaction appears, the trade room shows an amber **"Securing escrow"** badge and a confirmations bar ("3 of 10 · about 14 min"). After 10 confirmations (about 20 minutes) the badge turns teal: **"Escrow secured"**. The safest choice is to pay after this settles; a buyer who trusts the seller may pay sooner. **The buyer's 90-minute payment timer starts when the escrow settles**, so waiting never costs the buyer time.
7. **Pay and prove.** Within 90 minutes, the buyer:
   - sends the fiat,
   - uploads a proof screenshot (blurred in-app, metadata stripped; see 4.2),
   - taps "I've paid", which opens a confirmation step: "☐ I sent exactly $412.50 via Zelle with 4821 in the memo", then **Yes, I've paid** or **Not yet**. This stops accidental taps.

   Proof is **optional**. Neither LocalMonero nor Haveno required it, and cash trades often have nothing to screenshot. Without proof, the confirmation step adds "Sending without proof makes disputes harder." Confirming notifies the seller automatically (an alert, plus a system card in the chat with the amount, memo and any proof attached), so a chat message is optional.
8. **Confirm.** The seller checks their own bank or app, then taps "Received". The confirmation window depends on the payment method: about 2 hours for instant apps and up to 24 hours for bank wires.
9. **Release.** The seller ticks "I checked my bank account myself", then **presses and holds** the release button for 1.5 seconds. A fill sweeps across the button, and letting go early cancels. The seller's app signs, and the buyer's app co-signs automatically. The XMR reaches the buyer within seconds.
10. **Released screen.** Shows where the XMR went (for example "your external wallet ending …k3Fz") and live confirmations (1 of 10) from X2F's own node. An optional "View on the Monero blockchain" link opens a public explorer, with a note that explorers show the transaction and confirmations but never the amount or recipient.
11. **Review.** Both parties are prompted to review immediately (see section 5).

### 2.3 Cancelling and disputes
- **Mutual cancel ("abandon ship").** Either side proposes a cancel, and the other accepts. The two apps co-sign a refund: XMR back to the seller and any deposit back to the buyer. There is no moderator and no penalty. Profiles show a neutral cancel rate as information, not punishment.
- **Cancel after "I've paid".** The buyer sees a strong warning to cancel only if their payment was never sent or was returned. Scammers try to talk buyers into cancelling after they have paid.
- **Extend the timer.** When the payment window ends, the seller can give the buyer 30 more minutes instead of cancelling, at most twice per trade.
- **Buyer no-show: automatic refund.** If the 90-minute window ends and the buyer never tapped "I've paid", the seller taps "Cancel & get refund".
  - The buyer gets a final 15-minute alert: "The seller is cancelling. If you already paid, tap I've paid now."
  - If the buyer stays silent, the moderator key signs the refund automatically with the seller's key. No human and no dispute are involved.
  - This automatic path can only send the coins to the seller's own refund address, fixed when the escrow was set up.
  - **Dispute after refund.** If the buyer sent the fiat anyway, they can still open a dispute after the refund completes. The escrow is closed by then, so the moderator cannot move coins. The moderator can place a **trading hold** on the seller while the dispute is open, which blocks new ads and new trades. (X2F cannot freeze a user's coins: their wallet keys are on their device.) The moderator then rules on the evidence. If the payment reached the seller, the seller must **complete the trade as agreed**, sending the buyer the agreed XMR amount directly with an in-app Monero payment proof the moderator can verify. The agreement is enforced as made: neither side can switch it to a fiat refund. Only if the seller provably cannot send the XMR may the moderator order the fiat returned instead. The ruling is recorded on the seller's profile, and a seller who refuses is suspended and may be banned for life. The seller is told about this possibility before cancelling.
- **Seller no-show.** If the seller misses the confirmation window after the buyer marked paid, the buyer can open a dispute.
- **Dispute.** The moderator reviews the chat, the proofs and (on request) the unblurred proof, then signs with the winning side.
  - Each dispute has a published deadline and a live status.
  - Each ruling records the evidence behind it.
  - Either side can appeal to a second moderator.
  - **Trading hold.** During any dispute, a moderator can pause the other party's account from posting ads or starting trades until it is resolved. Trades already in progress continue under their own escrow. A hold never touches anyone's coins. New accounts start with low trade limits and no reputation, so dodging a hold with a fresh account still costs the user their standing.

### 2.4 Nudge
A Nudge (like ICQ's "Buzz") wakes up a counterparty who has dozed off, so traders who are online all night don't miss a deal.
- **What it does.** A Nudge button in a chat or trade sends one loud sound and a "Wake up, <name> wants to trade" alert to the other person's devices.
- **Settings.** One toggle turns Nudges on or off; it is on by default. Users can also set optional quiet hours (for example 1am to 7am).
- **Limits.**
  - Only someone with an open chat or active trade can nudge you.
  - One nudge per sender every 10 minutes.
  - At most 3 nudges received per hour from everyone combined.
  - Blocking someone blocks their nudges.
- **Privacy.** The push notification shows only "Someone nudged you on X2F", never names, amounts or trade details, because pushes pass through Apple's and Google's servers.
- **Platform limits.**
  - Android app: can use an alarm-style channel that sounds even on silent.
  - iPhone app: follows the ring and silent switch, since bypassing it needs a special Apple permission.
  - Website: plays the sound only while the tab is open; otherwise it shows a normal notification.

### 2.5 Buyer deposit (trust-scaled)
- No deposit for small first trades.
- Larger trades, and buyers without a track record, post a deposit into the same escrow.
- A buyer who ghosts after funding loses the deposit to the seller.

Thresholds are listed under open decisions (section 11).

### 2.6 Payment method profiles
Every payment method has a profile that drives all copy about it, so the app never describes a method wrongly ("arrived in Zelle"). Each profile holds:
- **What it is**, in one plain sentence. Example: "Zelle: a US bank-to-bank instant transfer built into most US banking apps."
- **Where the money lands and where to check**, such as "your bank account, checked in your bank's app". This text fills every "check your…" prompt.
- **Typical arrival time**, which sets the seller's confirmation window.
- **Matching fields**, such as Zelle email or phone, IBAN, or a cash-by-mail address. These drive the payment details card and the smart proof card.

The method name in a trade header opens its "What is this?" explainer.

X2F does not rate payment methods by risk. It can't know the real risk of every method in every country, so it makes no such claims.

**Currency matching comes from live offers, never from a hand-made list.** Every offer states its method and currency (for example "Zelle · USD"). The Sell and Buy method lists show:
- **"Buyers paying in USD"** (or the chosen currency): only methods with at least one live offer in that currency, sorted by Most buyers (default), Best rate or A–Z.
- **"All payment methods"**: every method in the catalog, A–Z, with no claim about which currencies it supports. Rows read "No USD buyers right now · post an offer".

The method catalog lives in `data/payment-methods.json`. It is seeded with 78 fiat methods named in Haveno's open-source list (names only, no code copied) plus "Custom method". Each entry must be checked against the provider's own website before launch. The catalog is a living list. Users can suggest missing methods, and "Custom method" covers anything not yet listed.

## 3. Escrow design

### 3.1 2-of-3 multisig
Each trade gets a fresh 2-of-3 Monero multisig wallet. The buyer, the seller and the moderator each hold one key, and any two can move the funds.

| Outcome | Signers |
|---|---|
| Normal release | seller + buyer |
| Mutual cancel | seller + buyer (refund to seller) |
| Dispute, buyer wins | moderator + buyer |
| Dispute, seller wins | moderator + seller |
| Buyer no-show (automatic) | moderator service + seller, refund address only |

Hard rules:
- **X2F holds at most one key per trade** (the moderator key).
- **No automatic third key.** An extra server key would give X2F two keys and make it custodial.

### 3.2 Proven so far
A feasibility test on 2026-10-14 used monero-ts 0.11.16 and monerod v0.18.5.3 on a private regtest chain. It passed all five checks:
1. normal release,
2. mutual cancel,
3. buyer-wins dispute,
4. seller-wins dispute,
5. one key alone cannot spend ("Not enough signers").

Funding took under 2 s and release about 3 s.

**Phase 0 passed the same evening (GO).** The escrow ran in ordinary web pages: the buyer in desktop Chrome, the seller in Firefox on an Android 14 phone, over home Wi-Fi through a message relay. Normal release and mutual cancel passed with the phone signing (as builder, then as co-signer); a moderator dispute passed in the desktop run. On the phone, escrow setup took about 17 s (4 message rounds) and building or co-signing a release about 2–3 s. Details: `spikes/phase0-browser/RESULTS.md`.

What Phase 0 taught the design:
- **Resume, don't fail.** Phones pause background pages; one run failed mid-release when the user switched apps. Every escrow step must be saved and retried or resumed, and the app asks users to keep X2F open during signing.
- **Wallet links work on Android.** A `monero:` link opened Cake Wallet with the address and amount filled in. iPhone and Edge are still to check.
- **Desktop funding is copy-paste or QR.** Linux has no handler for `monero:` links (Feather does not register one).
- **The node proxy serves Monero's normal paths at its root**, because monero-ts keeps only the host and port of the node address.
- Still to prove: iPhone Safari, the open internet and Tor, stagenet with real block times, many trades at once.

### 3.3 Protocol rules
- **Message relay.** Escrow setup and signing messages travel through X2F's relay server, end-to-end encrypted between participants. The relay cannot read or forge them.
- **Authenticated messages.** Every protocol message is signed by the sender's trade identity key and checked against the trade's participant list. This defends against the arbitrator-impersonation flaw behind the 2026 RetoSwap hacks.
- **Moderator key custody.**
  - The moderation service generates the moderator key for each trade and stores it encrypted at rest.
  - Disputes require a human moderator's decision.
  - Trades above a set threshold need two moderators to approve.
  - **One automatic exception: the buyer no-show refund (2.3).** It applies only when all three conditions hold:
    - the payment window has expired,
    - the buyer never marked paid,
    - the 15-minute final alert has passed.

    It can only sign a transaction paying the seller's refund address, which was recorded at escrow setup. Any other destination is refused, even if the service is compromised.
- **Every release is checked.** Each app independently verifies the destination address and amount before signing.

### 3.4 Monero upgrade risk
Monero's planned FCMP++ upgrade changes multisig. Multisig is unavailable on its test network today, and there is no mainnet date. The escrow layer sits behind one internal interface, so it can be swapped when the upgrade ships. We track the upgrade and stay on Monero's current multisig until the new version has been tested on the test network.

## 4. Privacy

### 4.1 Data rules
- **No identity checks** by X2F. No email or phone needed.
- **No analytics or third-party trackers.** No third-party scripts at all.
- **Trade chats and proofs are deleted** a set time after a trade closes (default 90 days, longer only while a dispute is open).
- **Disputed trades are kept 30 days** after the dispute closes, for moderator training, then deleted (decided 2026-10-17).
- **Anonymized training copies are kept 6 months**, then deleted (decided 2026-10-17).
- **No IP addresses are logged**, including during disputes (decided 2026-10-17).
- **Tor.** A `.onion` address for the web app. Tor is built into the native apps' networking. Users may also point the wallet at their own Monero node.

### 4.2 Proof uploads
- **Metadata stripped on the device** before upload: GPS, device model, timestamps. The image is re-encoded so nothing survives.
- **Automatic blur, then the buyer checks.** On the device, the app detects and pre-blurs likely private details: account and card numbers, full names, addresses, phone numbers and email addresses. The buyer reviews the result, then adds or removes blur with the in-app tool before sending. The upload screen says plainly that automatic blurring is best-effort and the buyer should double-check. The seller sees the blurred version.
- **Unblurred copy on request.** The buyer provides it only when a moderator asks during a dispute.
- **Smart proof card.** The app tries to read the amount, date, reference and payer name from the screenshot and flags mismatches, such as the wrong amount or a name that doesn't match the buyer. The card always reminds the seller to check their own bank before releasing; the proof is for disputes, not a substitute for checking.

## 5. Reviews and reputation

Reputation is how traders decide who to trust, so it is a core feature.

### 5.1 Review prompt
- **When:** the prompt appears the moment a trade completes.
- **What:** stars from 1 to 5, plus tap-to-add phrases. A note is optional, one line.
  - Good: Fast payment, Friendly, Clear instructions, Would trade again.
  - Bad: Slow, Went quiet, Asked for ID, Payment didn't match.
- **Required:** a user cannot start their next trade until they have rated the last one. A small "Neutral" button lets them clear it quickly without lying.

### 5.2 Platform feedback ("How's X2F doing?")
- **When:** after the trader rating is submitted, the same screen shows a thank-you and an optional "How's X2F doing?" card. It is never stacked with the required rating, and never required itself.
- **What:** one tap (Great, OK, Needs work) plus an optional free-text note ("Anything we could do better?"), then Send feedback or Not now.
- **How often:** after a user's first trade, then about every 10th trade, so it doesn't become noise.
- **Privacy:** sent without the username or trade details.

### 5.3 Fairness
- **Blind reveal.** Neither review shows until both are submitted, or 7 days pass. This stops revenge reviews.
- **Real trades only.** Reviews come only from completed escrow trades, one per trade. Larger trades carry more weight.
- **Disputed trades** can be reviewed once the dispute is resolved.

### 5.4 Profiles
- **Stats:** trades completed, % positive, typical release time, cancel rate, member since.
- **Badges are milestones.** They are earned automatically and can never be claimed or bought.
  - **New:** fewer than 5 completed trades (a safety badge).
  - **Trade milestones:** 10, 50, 100, 500 and 1,000 completed trades.
  - **Time:** Veteran at 1 year, then 3 years and 5 years.
  - **Quality:**
    - Trusted: 100+ trades and 98%+ positive.
    - Fast: usually pays or releases within 10 minutes.
    - Clean record: 100 trades with no lost disputes.
  - **Security honours:** PGP verified (signs in with a verified PGP key) and Security key verified (protects the account with a hardware key).
  - There are deliberately **no volume badges** (such as "traded $100k"). They would advertise how much money someone moves.
- **Badge visibility.** Each trader sets every earned badge, in Profile, Badges, to one of three levels:
  - **Featured:** shown on offer cards, at most 2.
  - **On hover:** shown only in the "+N" popup, each with a one-line meaning.
  - **Hidden:** shown to no one.
- **New is fixed:** it is always Featured and can't be hidden or moved. Achievements may be hidden; warnings may not.
- **With no choices made,** the defaults are Trusted, then the highest trade milestone, then Fast, then Veteran; the rest go On hover.
- **Badge style.** Every badge is the same pill, in teal (amber for New), with a small matching icon: New sprout, Trusted shield-check, Fast bolt, Veteran star, PGP key, Security key USB key.
- **Portable reputation:** a signed record of completed trades that the user owns and can export, signed with their PGP key when they have one. It survives even if X2F disappears.

## 6. Accounts and security

### 6.1 Account creation
There is no sign-up form at the front door; browsing needs no account. The first time a user taps Trade or posts an ad, a five-step setup runs while the trade waits (screens in the canvas's Getting started row):
1. **Pick a username.** 5–20 characters: letters, numbers, dots, dashes, underscores. Shown exactly as typed. No email or phone number, ever.
2. **Choose a login method** (6.2).
3. **Set it up:** paste a PGP public key and pass the PGP challenge, or register a passkey or security key, or set a password.
4. **Add a backup login (optional, strongly recommended).** The screen says plainly that X2F has no email or phone number, so a lost key or forgotten password means nobody can get the user back in; their coins stay safe in their own wallet, but the account and its reputation would be gone. "Skip for now" is allowed. *Decided 2026-10-14: the owner chose optional over mandatory. LocalMonero only ever asked for a password, and X2F holds no coins, so a lost account costs reputation, not money.*
5. **Link a wallet** to be paid to or to fund from (6.4). The wallet works immediately.
6. **Welcome screen.** "Welcome to X2F, <username>", a summary of how they log in, their backup and where they get paid, and one button: **Start trading**.

### 6.2 Login methods
Users may use any one method and add more later in Settings. All are mature, standard technology.
- **PGP login (recommended, shown first).** Sold to users as: no password to remember, and nothing for anyone to steal from X2F, which only holds the public key. Every later login is three steps: copy the message, decrypt it in a PGP app, paste the code.
  - The user pastes their public key; its fingerprint becomes the account ID.
  - X2F replies with an encrypted message holding a one-time login code. The user decrypts it and pastes back the code.
  - Each code is random, single-use and expires in 5 minutes.
  - The decrypted message names the site and the time, so a relayed phishing challenge is visible.
  - The message is signed with the official X2F PGP key, so the user's PGP app shows it really came from X2F; a missing or bad signature means stop (owner, 2026-10-17). The X2F signing key's fingerprint is published on the site and in the FAQ.
  - X2F never creates PGP keys for users, because that would expose the private key; the FAQ recommends PGP apps (owner, 2026-10-17).
  - Repeated failures slow down and then lock briefly.
  - Accepted keys: RSA 4096+, Ed25519, Ed448. Expired or revoked keys are turned away with the reason.
  - A "What's PGP?" explainer links to Kleopatra, GPG Suite and OpenKeychain, and says plainly that the PGP app is needed at each login.
- **Passkey:** WebAuthn on the device (SimpleWebAuthn on the server). Synced by Apple or Google; X2F learns nothing from that.
- **Hardware security key:** FIDO2 / WebAuthn, such as YubiKey, through the same code path. Several keys can be registered.
- **Password.** For users who just want a simple password, and the screen says that is fine. At least 10 characters with upper and lower case, a number and a symbol (owner's rule, 2026-10-14); refused if on a leaked-password list held on X2F's own server (no outside lookup); stored only as an Argon2id hash; rate-limited. Password users are nudged to add an authenticator app.
- **TOTP:** 6-digit codes that change every 30 seconds, from any authenticator app. A second factor or a backup, never the only login.
- **Backup method (optional):** a second PGP key, a security key, TOTP or printed recovery codes.
- **To verify when building the native apps:** passkeys inside the Capacitor wrappers need the app linked to X2F's domain and possibly a passkey plugin.

### 6.3 Optional hardening (Settings → Security)
- Require a security-key tap to fund escrow, withdraw or log in.
- **24-hour wait for new payout wallets (opt-in).** Under Security: "Make new payout addresses wait 24 hours." When it is on, the app warns that a fresh address per trade will also wait, so users should save a few in advance. *Decided 2026-10-14: opt-in, not default, so users can paste a fresh address per trade.*
- Daily spending limits.
- PGP-signed withdrawal addresses.
- Device list with remote log-out, and alerts for new logins.
- PGP-encrypted notifications for users who opt into email.

### 6.4 Your wallets and escrow keys
**Decided 2026-10-14: X2F has no built-in wallet.** The owner wants X2F to be purely a facilitator, never a "money man".
- **Your wallets.** Users link the Monero wallets they already use. Add a wallet: pick the app (Cake Wallet, Edge, Feather, Monerujo, Stack, Monero GUI/CLI, Ledger or Trezor through Feather or the GUI, Other), then paste or scan a receive address from it. X2F checks the address is a valid Monero address on the right network. The list shows each wallet's name and the end of its address, never a balance. **Adding a payout address requires confirming it's you** (a fresh PGP code, passkey, security key, or password plus any second factor), so a hijacked session cannot change it, and the app always shows an alert when one is added. Users who want more can turn on the 24-hour wait (6.3).
- **Edge privacy tip.** Edge has long synced Monero through servers that saw users' view keys; it now offers Full Sync on the phone. The Add a wallet step for Edge says: "For best privacy, turn on Full Sync in Edge's Monero settings."
- **Funding.** "Open in <wallet>" uses a standard `monero:` payment link carrying the escrow address and exact amount. QR code and copy-address are the fallback, and the only option on desktop. **To verify in Phase 0:** Cake Wallet and Edge open `monero:` links and fill in the amount correctly on iOS and Android.
- **Payouts** go to the wallet the buyer picks when the trade starts; refunds go to the seller's saved wallet.
- **Address reuse.** Monero addresses never expire and can be paid any number of times; on chain each payment lands at a one-time stealth address, so reuse is not visible publicly. The counterparty does see the payout address, so the app offers an optional tip: "For extra privacy, paste a fresh address from your wallet for each trade." Exchange deposit addresses are the exception, since the exchange controls them.
- **What "connect" means here.** Monero has no MetaMask-style connect standard; WalletConnect in Cake and Edge covers other chains, not Monero, and no phone wallet can join a multisig. So "connecting" a wallet means saving its address and opening it to pay.
- **Per-trade escrow keys.** The escrow still needs a buyer key and a seller key, so the app creates **one temporary key per trade** on the device. It never holds a balance of its own, is encrypted on the device, and is deleted once the trade closes. It is backed up **encrypted to the user's own PGP key** so a lost phone mid-trade can be recovered on a new device; X2F cannot read it.
- **Future goal.** If a Monero wallet ever supports a connect standard with multisig signing, hand escrow signing to the user's own wallet and drop the in-app key.

### 6.5 Application security
- **Target standard:** OWASP ASVS Level 3.
- **Tamper defence.** The main risk for a web wallet is a compromised server sending tampered code. Defences:
  - a strict Content Security Policy,
  - no third-party scripts,
  - Subresource Integrity on every asset,
  - pinned and audited dependencies with a minimal dependency count,
  - signed, reproducible native builds,
  - large trades nudged toward the native app.
- **Encryption:**
  - trade chat is encrypted to the buyer, the seller and the trade's moderator key, so a moderator can read it once a dispute opens (6.6),
  - TLS everywhere,
  - the `.onion` site for Tor users.
- **Tooling.** Every change is scanned with Semgrep, Claude Security and Security Guidance; supply-chain checks and `npm audit` must be clean.
  - The feasibility test already flagged a high and a moderate advisory in monero-ts's transitive dependencies. These must be resolved before production.
- **Before launch:** an independent code and cryptography audit, a penetration test, then a public bug bounty.

### 6.6 Staff, moderators and the owner account
Agreed with the owner 2026-10-15. Screens: canvas row "Owner & staff tools · desktop".

**First-run owner setup.** A new server has no owner, so the server prints a one-time setup code in its own log on first start; the setup page asks for it before anything else. The owner then picks a username, registers **two hardware security keys** (one kept as a spare) and a **PGP key**. Every owner login needs both a security key and the PGP key (decided 2026-10-17). There is no password and no reset by message. The page then locks permanently; there is only ever one owner. The staff tools live on a separate staff address.

**Roles.**
| Role | Can do |
|---|---|
| Owner | Add, remove and switch off staff; set login hours, limits and pay; see the full activity log; co-approve rulings at or above the large-trade limit |
| Senior moderator | Handle disputes and appeals; co-approve rulings at or above the limit; freeze or ban accounts |
| Moderator | Handle disputes; rule alone below the limit; larger rulings wait for a senior moderator or the owner |
| Helper | Answer questions and support tickets; no access to dispute evidence; cannot rule |

At launch only the owner and one moderator exist, and only the owner co-approves large rulings; senior moderators are added once trusted and trained.

**Adding staff.** The owner picks a role and login hours, and X2F creates a one-time invite link (expires in 24 hours) sent privately. The invitee sets up a staff login with a security key or PGP; staff cannot use passwords. Staff accounts are separate from any trading account. The owner can switch anyone off instantly; their past actions stay in the log.

**Login hours.** For each staff member the owner chooses the days, the start and end time and the time zone they may log in. Outside the window they cannot log in, and an open session ends when the window closes. The owner account has no time limit.

**Settings (owner only).** Large-trade limit (USD equivalent), first-response deadline for disputes, appeal window, and staff pay rates. Every change is logged.

**Pay.** Proposed model: per case for moderators and senior moderators, a flat monthly rate for helpers, paid monthly in XMR; a ruling overturned on appeal earns nothing. Amounts not set; the lawyer is consulted before the first payment.

**Moderators and chats.** Trade chats are encrypted so outsiders, the network and anyone who steals X2F's database cannot read them, but a moderator can read the whole chat (including pre-trade chat) once a dispute is opened on that trade. This is a rule enforced by the system plus the log, not a cryptographic barrier. Every chat each trade shows: "Chats are encrypted. If a dispute is opened, a moderator can read this conversation."

**Trade codes.** Every trade gets a random 16-character code in four groups, for example `K7M2-9QXA-4FTR-8WPN`, using letters and digits without look-alikes (Crockford base32: no I, L, O, U). Codes never count up, so they reveal nothing about trade volume. The code is the trade's ID everywhere: the trade header, the payment memo, support and the log.

**Activity log.** Every staff action, including the owner's, and every step of every trade is recorded and cannot be edited or deleted: funding, payment marked, releases, disputes, opening a dispute chat, requesting an unblurred proof, proposing and approving rulings, invites, setting changes, sessions ended by login hours. Typing a trade code (with or without dashes), or picking one from the **Active disputes** dropdown (every open dispute with its payment method, amount and status), shows every entry for that trade and nothing else.

**Owner overview (owner only).**
- **Emergency stop.** One button pauses every new trade and offer site-wide, confirmed by a security-key tap. Trades already in escrow can still be released, refunded or disputed, so no coins get stuck. Every user sees a notice until the owner resumes (another key tap).
- **Earnings.** Fee income this month, last month and all time, in XMR with a USD estimate and trade counts. The 1% fee goes to X2F's fee wallet; the server reads it with a view-only key, and the spending key stays offline.
- **Announcement banner.** A message (up to 140 characters) shown at the top of every screen for every user, as Information (teal) or Warning (amber), with a live preview. The owner can change or remove it at any time.
- **Maintenance mode.** For planned upgrades: users see the owner's message instead of the app, and escrow timers pause until it is switched off.

**Moderator performance (owner only).** Per staff member over 30 days, 90 days or all time: cases handled, typical (median) first-reply time, typical time to ruling, rulings overturned on appeal, share of replies within the deadline, and open cases. Used for coaching, pay and promotion to senior moderator. A **Show chart** button on each row opens that person's last 8 weeks as charts (cases per week as bars; typical first reply per week as a line against the 4-hour deadline), with summary numbers, a hover readout and a **Show as table** option.

**Legal requests.** What X2F stores and what it would hand over to a government request is set with the owner's lawyer before launch. Storing little is the main protection.

**Training library.** Every dispute is saved and logged. The owner can mark a closed dispute as a training case; X2F makes an anonymized copy (Buyer and Seller instead of usernames; names, bank details and wallet addresses masked) and the original is kept 30 days after the dispute closes (4.1), then deleted. The anonymized copy is kept 6 months, then deleted. Trainees only ever see anonymized copies.

**Moderator badge.** A violet shield with "X2F Moderator", a colour no other element uses. Traders only see it inside a disputed trade's room, drawn by the app from server records, with a banner when a moderator joins and a ruling card with the outcome and reason. Moderators never contact traders anywhere else about a dispute.

**Still to design:** the moderator dashboard (queue, case view, ruling tools), the trader's view of a dispute with a moderator present, and the in-app Help page and staff list. Community room plans are parked in `docs/community-room-plan.md`.

## 8. Architecture

| Part | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| App | TypeScript, React, Vite; installable PWA | One codebase, mature tooling |
| Native wrappers | Capacitor (iOS, Android) | Wraps the same web code; adds secure storage and Tor networking |
| Escrow keys | monero-ts (official Monero code compiled to WebAssembly) in the client | Per-trade escrow keys only, no user wallet; keys never leave the device; proven in the feasibility test |
| PGP | OpenPGP.js | Standard library with independent audits |
| Passkeys and security keys | WebAuthn | Built into browsers and phones |
| E2E encryption | libsodium | Mature and audited |
| Backend API | TypeScript (Fastify) + PostgreSQL | Ads, auth challenges, reputation, disputes; holds no user funds |
| Relay | WebSocket relay for chat and escrow messages | Passes only encrypted, signed messages |
| Monero nodes | X2F-run nodes (clearnet and .onion); users may set their own | Escrow sync and confirmations |
| Price feed | Median of several exchange sources | No single source can skew prices |

Escrow logic sits behind one interface (`EscrowEngine`), so the multisig implementation can change without touching the trade flow.

## 9. Testing

- **Unit tests** for pricing, fees, timers, the trade state machine and review rules.
- **Escrow integration tests** on a private regtest chain, extending the feasibility test: every outcome in 3.1, timeouts, a lost device, and messages forged or replayed.
- **End-to-end tests** with Playwright for full trades between two browser sessions, on phone and desktop sizes.
- **Security tests:**
  - fuzzing of protocol messages,
  - static analysis on every change,
  - dependency audits,
  - an external audit and penetration test before launch.

## 10. Phases

0. **Proof in the browser.** Rerun the escrow test with monero-ts in a real browser and on a phone, measuring setup time over a real network.
1. **Design.** X2F design system, then the screens in the order of section 7.
2. **MVP (web).** Trade loop, escrow, reviews, PGP, passkey, security key, password and TOTP login with an optional backup, metadata stripping and the blur tool, moderator dashboard, Monero test network only.
3. **Hardening.** Security keys, allowlists, PGP-encrypted backups, Tor `.onion`, then the external audit and penetration test.
4. **Launch.** Mainnet with trade limits, a bug bounty, then the native app wrappers.

## 11. Open decisions

These need answers before or during the MVP. Each has a proposed default.

1. **Business model and fees. Decided 2026-10-14:** a flat **1%** fee on completed trades only, paid **only by the ad poster**; the person taking an ad pays no X2F fee. The ad poster learns of it at sign-up and in the terms of service, and sees it itemised on the released screen ("X2F fee (1%, your ad) −0.012500 XMR" above "You receive"); it is never shown to the person taking the ad. It is taken as a second output of the escrow release: added to the escrow when the ad poster is the seller, deducted from the payout when the ad poster is the buyer. Cancelled and refunded trades pay nothing. **Monero network fees** (decided 2026-10-17): the seller pays the funding transaction's fee from their own wallet; the release or refund transaction's fee comes out of the payout to whoever receives the XMR, never out of the X2F fee output. **Extra XMR sent to an escrow** (decided 2026-10-17): anything beyond the funded amount, even dust, goes to the **ad poster** at the end of the trade, like a tip; it is paid as its own output (or added to their payout when they are the one receiving) and the X2F fee output stays exact. Overpaying should not happen, because funding uses a `monero:` link or QR code with the exact amount filled in. Proposed (not yet decided): if more dust arrives and stays locked, settlement waits up to about 1 hour, then pays out the unlocked amount. For comparison: LocalMonero charged 1% to the ad poster; Hodl Hodl 0.75% to each side; Peach 2% to the buyer; RoboSats 0.2% split.
2. **Legal and jurisdiction.** Even non-custodial platforms have faced regulators (Tornado Cash conviction, UK FCA warning about LocalCoinSwap). Proposed: get a crypto-specialist lawyer's opinion before mainnet launch, and choose the operating country on that advice.
3. **Moderators.** Roles, tools and login hours are decided (6.6). Still open: pay amounts and the legal form of paying staff.
4. **Deposit thresholds.** Proposed: no deposit under $200 equivalent for buyers with fewer than 3 trades; a 15% deposit above that.
5. **Launch payment methods.** Proposed: cash by mail, bank transfer (SEPA/ACH), Zelle, Revolut, Wise, plus a "Custom" method.
6. **Price feed sources** and how to handle exchanges delisting XMR.
7. **Sanctioned payment systems.** SBP, Mir and YooMoney (Russia) are in the catalog but flagged; a lawyer must sign off before any sanctioned rail goes live.
8. **Gift cards.** Allowed or not (Amazon eGift and Paysafecard are in the catalog).
