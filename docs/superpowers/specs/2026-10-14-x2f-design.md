**Keeping and deleting trades** [1, 4.1, 6.6]

- **A disputed trade is kept 90 days, counted from the moment someone presses ~~the dispute button~~ the dispute closing (owner p11-11, 2026-11-09).** Only disputes are kept longer, because they are what moderators train on.
- **No per-trade stub.** Nothing about a deleted trade is kept per trade. What must last is kept as running totals on the account (completed trades and ratings; a lost-disputes total is a lead addition, for the Clean record badge) and in the revenue ledger. **Disputes and appeals** [2.3, 6.6]
- **Disputes are settled within 48 hours; the goal is 24 hours or less.**
- **A settled dispute still counts as a completed trade,** whoever won (money most likely changed hands, and disputes are common). There is **no automatic stage drop** for a lost dispute. Instead, a moderator can **recommend account limits to a senior moderator** for habitual offenders and obvious hit-and-run cases.
- **Appeals.** The dispute outcome notice says: "Don't agree with the moderator's decision? Request an appeal. You have 48 hours (until <exact date and time>)." The deadline is computed from the decision. A **File appeal** button shows on the completed-trades list and on that trade's page, and disappears when the 48 hours are up. *(2026-10-29c, owner: 48 hours everywhere; the earlier 72 is gone.)*
- **Who decides an appeal:** a senior moderator or the owner. **Nobody involved in the original dispute** (decided it, assisted, or commented) may take part in its appeal. This is enforced in code, not just policy.
- **A won appeal** (1) fixes the record (marks, ratings, limits) and (2) puts a **lien** on the other account: it loses all trading privileges until it repays the amount owed to the winner. The amount owed is the loss, the trade's XMR amount. Repayment goes through an **X2F repayment trade with escrow**; the system sees it complete and lifts the lien automatically. *2026-10-29c (owner): a lien also comes at once with a lost dispute that requires restitution, and is lifted while an appeal is pending; see Revision 2026-10-29c.*

## 2. The trade flow

### 2.1 Roles
- **Seller:** has XMR, wants fiat. Always the one who funds escrow.
- **Buyer:** has fiat, wants XMR.
- **Moderator:** an X2F staff member who holds the third escrow key and decides disputes.

Either side can post an ad: sellers post "selling XMR" ads, and buyers post "buying XMR" ads.

### 2.2 Step by step

1. **Browse.** Buy and sell boards show ads filtered by the payment methods and currency the user chooses, so they only see deals they can actually complete. Browsing needs no account.
2. **Chat.** A user may open free pre-trade chats with any number of counterparties. Chats cost nothing, carry no penalties and expire if ignored.
3. **Quote.** Picking an ad opens a quote screen. The seller enters an amount or taps Max. One screen then shows:
   - the XMR amount and the ad's rate,
   - the fiat total in both parties' currencies,
   - the network fee (always the fast priority),
   - no X2F fee line: the person taking an ad pays none. The ad poster's 1% is itemised on their released screen (see section 11, item 1),
   - one final total.

   Nothing changes after the user confirms.
4. **"Trade."** Whoever takes an offer taps **Trade** (a buyer taking a sell offer, or a seller taking a buy offer); the offer's poster is told "<username> accepted your offer". The buyer then picks **which of their own wallets gets paid**, from the wallets saved under Your wallets (see 6.4), or adds one there and then (an address from their wallet app; adding one asks the user to confirm it's them, see 6.4). The buyer's tap starts escrow setup between the apps and locks the buyer's deposit if this trade requires one (see 2.5). A buyer can have only one "I'm in" waiting for funding at a time per ad amount, so five sellers cannot fund five escrows for one buyer.
5. **Fund (the key turn).** The seller has **30 minutes** to fund from their own wallet. The main button opens their saved wallet app ("Open in Cake Wallet") with the escrow address and exact amount already filled in, so they only confirm; a QR code and a copyable address are the fallback. The window is longer than the 10 minutes first proposed because every seller now pays from an outside wallet. The price locks at this moment. **Offer pricing** (decided 2026-10-17): the poster picks either market price plus or minus a percentage (the default) or a fixed price per XMR, as Haveno does; the form warns that a fixed price goes stale when the market moves. **Guarding against paying twice** (owner, 2026-10-21): the funding button is press-and-hold (1.5 s, like Release) and works once; after one use it locks to "Waiting for your payment…". As soon as the full amount is seen, the funding screen switches to "Escrow funded" and the address, QR code, copy button and Open-in-wallet link are removed, so nothing is left to scan or tap again. A Monero address cannot refuse payments, so any extra that still arrives follows 11.1. **Funding is the commitment: once the seller taps "Fund escrow", the seller can never cancel** (owner, 2026-10-29b). Before that, either side can walk away. If the seller does not fund in time, the buyer is released with no penalty.
6. **Securing escrow.** Once the funding transaction appears, the trade room shows an amber **"Securing escrow"** badge and a confirmations bar ("3 of 10 · about 14 min"). After 10 confirmations (about 20 minutes) the badge turns teal: **"Escrow secured"**. The safest choice is to pay after this settles; a buyer who trusts the seller may pay sooner. **The buyer's 90-minute payment timer starts when the escrow settles**, so waiting never costs the buyer time.
7. **Pay and prove.** Within 90 minutes, the buyer:
   - sends the fiat,
   - uploads a proof screenshot (blurred in-app, metadata stripped; see 4.2),
   - taps "I've paid", which opens a confirmation step (owner, 2026-10-29b: two steps, to avoid accidental taps). The owner's question: "Are you sure you want to tell the seller you've sent their payment in the requested format?" (final words by Claude Design). The sheet has a **required tick** "I sent exactly $412.50 by Zelle with the memo K7M2-…", the line "After this you can't cancel the trade. If something goes wrong, open a dispute.", then **Yes, mark as paid** (enabled only after the tick) or **Not yet**.
   - The "don't pay until the escrow is complete" warning shows before "I've paid" and also when the pay screen opens while the escrow is still confirming (2026-10-29b).

   Proof is **optional**. Neither LocalMonero nor Haveno required it, and cash trades often have nothing to screenshot. Without proof, the confirmation step adds "Sending without proof makes disputes harder." Confirming notifies the seller automatically (an alert, plus a system card in the chat with the amount, memo and any proof attached), so a chat message is optional.
8. **Confirm.** The seller checks their own bank or app, then taps "Received". The confirmation window depends on the payment method: about 2 hours for instant apps and up to 24 hours for bank wires.
9. **Release.** The seller ticks "I checked my bank account myself", then **presses and holds** the release button for 1.5 seconds. A fill sweeps across the button, and letting go early cancels. The seller's app signs, and the buyer's app co-signs automatically. The XMR reaches the buyer within seconds.
10. **Released screen.** Shows where the XMR went (for example "your external wallet ending …k3Fz") and live confirmations (1 of 10) from X2F's own node. An optional "View on the Monero blockchain" link opens a public explorer, with a note that explorers show the transaction and confirmations but never the amount or recipient.
11. **Review.** Both parties are prompted to review immediately (see section 5).

- **Before funding** (owner, 2026-10-29b): either side can walk away; nothing is in escrow.
- **Mutual cancel ("abandon ship").** Either side proposes a cancel, and the other accepts (only before "I've paid", 2026-10-29b). The two apps co-sign a refund: XMR back to the seller. There is no moderator and no penalty. Profiles show a neutral cancel rate as information, not punishment.
- **Buyer's cancel after funding, with the seller's confirm** (owner, 2026-10-29c: option B). The buyer taps **Cancel trade** (the link keeps that name) any time after funding and before "I've paid". The XMR starts going back to the seller at once: the buyer's app signs and the moderator key signs automatically, only to the seller's refund address recorded at escrow setup, as in the no-show refund, so the seller need not be online. The seller's **Confirm cancel** button is greyed out until the return has all its confirmations (10); the seller's tap then officially cancels the trade. If the seller never taps, the trade stays in the seller's Active trades with "Your input is needed to cancel this trade." and closes by itself as cancelled 48 hours after the button became available (anchor: lead reading). A seller can never cancel after tapping "Fund escrow". **A cancel can't be disputed** (owner, 2026-10-29c): the escrow returns everything, so there is no wrong-amount risk.

Scammers try to talk buyers into cancelling after they have paid.

  - The buyer gets a final 15-minute alert: "The seller is cancelling. If you already paid, tap I've paid now."
  - If the buyer stays silent, the moderator key signs the refund automatically with the seller's key. No human and no dispute are involved.
  - This automatic path can only send the coins to the seller's own refund address, fixed when the escrow was set up.
  - **Dispute after refund.** If the buyer sent the fiat anyway, they can still open a dispute after the refund completes. The escrow is closed by then, so the moderator cannot move coins. The moderator can place a **trading hold** on the seller while the dispute is open, which blocks new ads and new trades. (X2F cannot freeze a user's coins: their wallet keys are on their device.) The moderator then rules on the evidence. If the payment reached the seller, the seller must **complete the trade as agreed**, sending the buyer the agreed XMR amount directly with an in-app Monero payment proof the moderator can verify. The agreement is enforced as made: neither side can switch it to a fiat refund. Only if the seller provably cannot send the XMR may the moderator order the fiat returned instead. The ruling is recorded on the seller's profile, and a seller who refuses is suspended and may be banned for life. - **Seller no-show.** If the seller misses the confirmation window after the buyer marked paid, the buyer can open a dispute.
- **Dispute.** The moderator reviews the chat, the proofs and (on request) the unblurred proof, then signs with the winning side.

  - Each ruling records the evidence behind it.
  - **Settled within 48 hours; the goal is 24 hours or less** (owner, 2026-10-29).
  - **Appeals** (owner, 2026-10-29). The outcome notice says "Don't agree with the moderator's decision? Request an appeal. You have 48 hours (until <exact date and time>)." A senior moderator or the owner decides; nobody involved in the original dispute (decided, assisted or commented) may take part, enforced in code.   - **Liens** (owner, 2026-10-29c; replaces the 29b conflict).     - **Starter:** 0–4 completed trades: up to $200 per trade.
    - **Building:** 5–19 completed trades and an account at least 7 days old: up to $1,000 per trade.
    - **Graduated:** 20+ completed trades and an account at least 30 days old: no platform cap.

    - At most 3 open trades at once while on Starter.
    - The owner can change every number in Settings; changes are logged. Ad posters can still be stricter (minimum completed trades on their ad).
    - The offer list shows the taker only what they can take (an offer's minimum above their cap is greyed out with "Opens at Building"). The amber **New** badge stays until Graduated.

### 2.4 Nudge
A Nudge (like ICQ's "Buzz") wakes up a counterparty who has dozed off, so traders who are online all night don't miss a deal.
- **What it does.** A Nudge button in a chat or trade sends one loud sound and a "Wake up, <name> wants to trade" alert to the other person's devices.
- **Settings.** One toggle turns Nudges on or off; it is on by default. Users can also set optional quiet hours (for example 1am to 7am).
- **Limits.**
  - Only someone with an open chat or active trade can nudge you.
  - One nudge per sender every 10 minutes.
  - At most 3 nudges received per hour from everyone combined.
  - Blocking someone blocks their nudges.
- **Blocking** (owner, 2026-10-29). Any trader can block another. The blocked person can't see your offers, take them, message you, or see that you're online. **Blocking is silent** (2026-10-29b): the blocked person is never told, and trades already open finish normally.
- **Privacy.** The push notification shows only "Someone nudged you on X2F", never names, amounts or trade details, because pushes pass through Apple's and Google's servers.
- **Platform limits.**
  - Android app: can use an alarm-style channel that sounds even on silent.
  - iPhone app: follows the ring and silent switch, since bypassing it needs a special Apple permission.
  - Website: plays the sound only while the tab is open; otherwise it shows a normal notification.

### 2.5 Buyer deposit
**No buyer deposits** (owner, 2026-10-17). Buyers never put XMR into the escrow; only the seller funds it. Many buyers have no XMR yet, and the cancel rules (2.3) and the no-show refund already protect sellers. Revisit only if no-shows become a real problem (an earlier idea: 1% on trades of $5,000 or more).

### 2.6 Payment method profiles
Every payment method has a profile that drives all copy about it, so the app never describes a method wrongly ("arrived in Zelle"). Each profile holds:
- **What it is**, in one plain sentence. Example: "Zelle: a US bank-to-bank instant transfer built into most US banking apps."
- **Where the money lands and where to check**, such as "your bank account, checked in your bank's app". This text fills every "check your…" prompt.
- **Typical arrival time**, which sets the seller's confirmation window.
- **Matching fields**, such as Zelle email or phone, IBAN, or a cash-by-mail address. These drive the payment details card and the smart proof card.

The method name in a trade header opens its "What is this?" explainer.

X2F does not rate payment methods by risk. It can't know the real risk of every method in every country, so it makes no such claims.

**Currency matching comes from live offers, never from a hand-made list.** Every offer states its method and currency (for example "Zelle · USD"). The Sell and Buy method lists show:
- **"Buyers paying in USD"** (or the chosen currency): only methods with at least one live offer in that currency, sorted by Most buyers (default), Best rate or A–Z.
- **"All payment methods"**: every method in the catalog, A–Z, with no claim about which currencies it supports. Rows read "No USD buyers right now · post an offer".

The method catalog lives in `data/payment-methods.json`. It is seeded with 78 fiat methods named in Haveno's open-source list (names only, no code copied) plus "Custom method". Each entry must be checked against the provider's own website before launch. The catalog is a living list. Users can suggest missing methods, and "Custom method" covers anything not yet listed.

## 3. Escrow design

### 3.1 2-of-3 multisig
Each trade gets a fresh 2-of-3 Monero multisig wallet. The buyer, the seller and the moderator each hold one key, and any two can move the funds.

| Outcome | Signers |
|---|---|
| Normal release | seller + buyer |
| Mutual cancel | seller + buyer (refund to seller) |
| Dispute, buyer wins | moderator + buyer |
| Dispute, seller wins | moderator + seller |
| Buyer no-show (automatic) | moderator service + seller, refund address only |
| Buyer cancels (automatic) | moderator service + buyer, seller's refund address only (option B, owner 2026-10-29c; the seller's "Confirm cancel" then closes the trade) |

Hard rules:
- **X2F holds at most one key per trade** (the moderator key).
- **No automatic third key.** An extra server key would give X2F two keys and make it custodial.

### 3.2 Proven so far
A feasibility test on 2026-10-14 used monero-ts 0.11.16 and monerod v0.18.5.3 on a private regtest chain. It passed all five checks:
1. normal release,
2. mutual cancel,
3. buyer-wins dispute,
4. seller-wins dispute,
5. one key alone cannot spend ("Not enough signers").

Funding took under 2 s and release about 3 s.

**Phase 0 passed the same evening (GO).** The escrow ran in ordinary web pages: the buyer in desktop Chrome, the seller in Firefox on an Android 14 phone, over home Wi-Fi through a message relay. Normal release and mutual cancel passed with the phone signing (as builder, then as co-signer); a moderator dispute passed in the desktop run. On the phone, escrow setup took about 17 s (4 message rounds) and building or co-signing a release about 2–3 s. Details: `spikes/phase0-browser/RESULTS.md`.

What Phase 0 taught the design:
- **Resume, don't fail.** Phones pause background pages; one run failed mid-release when the user switched apps. Every escrow step must be saved and retried or resumed, and the app asks users to keep X2F open during signing.
- **Wallet links work on Android.** A `monero:` link opened Cake Wallet with the address and amount filled in. iPhone and Edge are still to check.
- **Desktop funding is copy-paste or QR.** Linux has no handler for `monero:` links (Feather does not register one).
- **The node proxy serves Monero's normal paths at its root**, because monero-ts keeps only the host and port of the node address.
- Still to prove: iPhone Safari, the open internet and Tor, stagenet with real block times, many trades at once.

### 3.3 Protocol rules
- **Message relay.** Escrow setup and signing messages travel through X2F's relay server, end-to-end encrypted between participants. The relay cannot read or forge them.
- **Authenticated messages.** Every protocol message is signed by the sender's trade identity key and checked against the trade's participant list. This defends against the arbitrator-impersonation flaw behind the 2026 RetoSwap hacks.
- **Moderator key custody.**
  - The moderation service generates the moderator key for each trade and stores it encrypted at rest.
  - Disputes require a human moderator's decision.
  - Trades above a set threshold need two moderators to approve.
  - **Two automatic exceptions, both paying only the seller's refund address.** The first is the buyer no-show refund (2.3). It applies only when all three conditions hold:
    - the payment window has expired,
    - the buyer never marked paid,
    - the 15-minute final alert has passed.

    The second is the buyer's own cancel (2.3): the buyer has signed the cancel and the refund, before tapping "I've paid". It is refused once the buyer has marked paid (option B, owner 2026-10-29c). The service can only sign one transaction paying the whole balance to the seller's refund address, which was recorded at escrow setup. Any other destination is refused, even if the service is compromised.
- **Every release is checked.** Each app independently verifies the destination address and amount before signing.

### 3.4 Monero upgrade risk
Monero's planned FCMP++ upgrade changes multisig. Multisig is unavailable on its test network today, and there is no mainnet date. The escrow layer sits behind one internal interface, so it can be swapped when the upgrade ships. We track the upgrade and stay on Monero's current multisig until the new version has been tested on the test network.

## 4. Privacy

### 4.1 Data rules
- **No identity checks** by X2F. No email or phone needed.
- **No analytics or third-party trackers.** No third-party scripts at all.

## 5. Reviews and reputation

Reputation is how traders decide who to trust, so it is a core feature.

### 5.1 Review prompt
- **When:** the prompt appears the moment a trade completes.
- **What:** stars from 1 to 5, plus tap-to-add phrases. A note is optional, one line. *Owner, 2026-10-29:* a review is a custom comment, templated comments, badges that describe the trade, and a star rating (~~1–5 or 1–10, not decided~~ **1–5 only**, design reports the owner's answer, 2026-10-29b).
- **Permanent** (owner, 2026-10-29). Reviews survive the 30-day trade delete, as on Amazon or Uber. The person rated can **dispute a review** and have it moderated (retaliation, a competitor, doxxing and similar).
- **Public but anonymous** (owner, 2026-10-29b). Nothing on a review links it to whoever left it: no username, no account number, no order or trade number.
- **Under review** (owner, 2026-10-29b). A disputed review immediately shows "Under review" (the benefit of the doubt goes to the person reviewed) until a moderator removes some comments, lets it stand, or removes it entirely. Disputing is a "Dispute" button with a free-text reason; no reason list for now (design reports the owner's answer).
  - Good: Fast payment, Friendly, Clear instructions, Would trade again.
  - Bad: Slow, Went quiet, Asked for ID, Payment didn't match.
- **Required:** a user cannot start their next trade until they have rated the last one. A small "Neutral" button lets them clear it quickly without lying.

### 5.2 Platform feedback ("How's X2F doing?")
- **When:** after the trader rating is submitted, the same screen shows a thank-you and an optional "How's X2F doing?" card. It is never stacked with the required rating, and never required itself.
- **What:** one tap (Great, OK, Needs work) plus an optional free-text note ("Anything we could do better?"), then Send feedback or Not now.
- **How often:** after a user's first trade, then about every 10th trade, so it doesn't become noise.
- **Privacy:** sent without the username or trade details.

**Feedback link on every "can't do that" popup** (owner, 2026-10-29). Every popup that tells a user they can't do something carries "If you feel this feature can be improved please leave feedback". It opens a form: complaint, feedback or recommendation, with a "Send anonymously" checkbox (named feedback lets staff reach out for details). After sending: "Thank you! Each and every comment is appreciated and reviewed to help make this community a better place for us all."

### 5.3 Fairness
- **Blind reveal.** Neither review shows until both are submitted, or 7 days pass. This stops revenge reviews.
- **Real trades only.** Reviews come only from completed escrow trades, one per trade. Larger trades carry more weight.
- **Disputed trades** can be reviewed once the dispute is resolved.

### 5.4 Profiles
- **Stats:** trades completed, % positive, typical release time, cancel rate, member since, and **"Disputes: N won, M lost"** (2026-10-29c: lead recommendation, owner leaning yes). A lost dispute still counts as a completed trade (R38); the dispute screen's footer "It appears on your profile as a lost dispute" stays (lead recommendation, owner leaning yes).
- **Badges are milestones.** They are earned automatically and can never be claimed or bought.
  - **New:** shown until the account graduates from starter limits (§2.3, owner 2026-10-24); a safety badge that cannot be hidden.
  - **Trade milestones:** 10, 50, 100, 500 and 1,000 completed trades.
  - **Time:** Veteran at 1 year, then 3 years and 5 years.
  - **Quality:**
    - Trusted: 100+ trades and 98%+ positive.
    - Fast: usually pays or releases within 10 minutes.
    - Clean record: 100 trades with no lost disputes.
  - **Security honours:** PGP verified (signs in with a verified PGP key) and Security key verified (protects the account with a hardware key).
  - There are deliberately **no volume badges** (such as "traded $100k"). They would advertise how much money someone moves.

  - **Featured:** shown on offer cards, at most 2.

## 6. Accounts and security

### 6.1 Account creation
There is no sign-up form at the front door; browsing needs no account. The first time a user taps Trade or posts an ad, a short setup runs while the trade waits (screens in the canvas's Getting started row):
0. **Accept the Terms of Service.** The Terms screen comes first: the user must scroll to the end, tick the box and tap **I accept**. The server records which Terms version each account accepted; when the Terms change, the screen appears again at the next login and trading waits until the user accepts (owner, 2026-10-17).
1. **Pick a display name and a login name** (owner, 2026-10-23). The **display name** is public (offers, profiles, chat). The **login name** is private: never shown to anyone, used only to log in with a password or passkey, so nobody can target an account they see on the site. Both 5–20 characters: letters, numbers, dots, dashes, underscores; they must differ. No email or phone number, ever. **A deleted account's names are burned forever** (owner, 2026-10-29b): never reused, and no way to bring them back.
2. **Choose a login method** (6.2).
3. **Set it up:** paste a PGP public key and pass the PGP challenge, or register a passkey or security key, or set a password.
4. **Add a backup login (optional, strongly recommended).** The screen says plainly that X2F has no email or phone number, so a lost key or forgotten password means nobody can get the user back in; their coins stay safe in their own wallet, but the account and its reputation would be gone. "Skip for now" is allowed. *Decided 2026-10-14: the owner chose optional over mandatory. LocalMonero only ever asked for a password, and X2F holds no coins, so a lost account costs reputation, not money.*
5. **Link a wallet** to be paid to or to fund from (6.4). The wallet works immediately. The account's first wallet never waits, even when added later, after setup; only later wallets can (6.3, "Wallet rules refined").
6. **Welcome screen.** "Welcome to X2F, <username>", a summary of how they log in, their backup and where they get paid, and one button: **Start trading**.

### 6.2 Login methods
Users may use any one method and add more later in Settings. All are mature, standard technology.
- **PGP login (recommended, shown first).** Sold to users as: no password to remember, and nothing for anyone to steal from X2F, which only holds the public key. Every later login is three steps: copy the message, decrypt it in a PGP app, paste the code.
  - **PGP login needs no name at all** (owner, 2026-10-23): the user pastes their public key (or its fingerprint) and X2F sends the login message for that key. An unknown key gets a message that looks the same, so the login page never reveals whether a key has an account. The display name is the public trading name.
  - X2F replies with an encrypted message holding a one-time login code. The user decrypts it and pastes back the code.
  - Each code is random, single-use and expires in 5 minutes.
  - The decrypted message names the site and the time, so a relayed phishing challenge is visible.
  - The message is signed with the official X2F PGP key, so the user's PGP app shows it really came from X2F; a missing or bad signature means stop (owner, 2026-10-17). The X2F signing key's fingerprint is published on the site and in the FAQ.
  - **Email inside a PGP key is never recorded or used** (owner, 2026-10-17). Most keys carry a name and email (often fake or empty). Where the key still works without them (newer v6 keys), X2F removes them before storing; older v4 keys break if they are removed, so those are stored only encrypted and decrypted in memory just to send a login message. X2F never shows, logs, searches or exports anything inside a key. The key screen says: "If your key contains an email address, X2F never shows or uses it. For full privacy, use a key without one."
  - X2F never creates PGP keys for users, because that would expose the private key; the FAQ recommends PGP apps (owner, 2026-10-17).
  - Repeated failures slow down and then lock briefly. Codes themselves are not throttled per guess, because each is random (60 bits), single-use and expires in 5 minutes; only asking for new codes is rate-limited, so a stranger cannot lock the real user out (Plan 2, P2-C1).
  - Accepted keys: RSA 4096+, Ed25519, Ed448. Expired or revoked keys are turned away with the reason.
  - A "What's PGP?" explainer links to Kleopatra, GPG Suite and OpenKeychain, and says plainly that the PGP app is needed at each login.
- **Passkey:** WebAuthn on the device (SimpleWebAuthn on the server). Synced by Apple or Google; X2F learns nothing from that.
- **Hardware security key:** FIDO2 / WebAuthn, such as YubiKey, through the same code path. Several keys can be registered.
- **Password.** For users who just want a simple password, and the screen says that is fine. At least 10 characters with upper and lower case, a number and a symbol (owner's rule, 2026-10-14); refused if on a leaked-password list held on X2F's own server (no outside lookup); stored only as an Argon2id hash; rate-limited. Password users are nudged to add an authenticator app.
- **TOTP:** 6-digit codes that change every 30 seconds, from any authenticator app. A second factor or a backup, never the only login.
- **Backup method (optional):** a second PGP key, a security key, a passkey (owner, 2026-10-17), TOTP or printed recovery codes. A password never counts as a backup.
- **No patterns in secrets** (owner, 2026-10-17). Every TOTP secret (160 bits) and every recovery code (80 bits each) comes straight from the operating system's secure random generator, fresh for each customer and each time they are made. Nothing is derived from the username, account ID, time, a counter or a shared seed, so knowing one customer's codes says nothing about another's, or about the same customer's next set. The server stores TOTP secrets encrypted and recovery codes only as hashes.
- **To verify when building the native apps:** passkeys inside the Capacitor wrappers need the app linked to X2F's domain and possibly a passkey plugin.

### 6.4 Your wallets and escrow keys
**Decided 2026-10-14: X2F has no built-in wallet.** The owner wants X2F to be purely a facilitator, never a "money man".
- **Your wallets.** Users link the Monero wallets they already use. Add a wallet: pick the app (Cake Wallet, Edge, Feather, Monerujo, Stack, Monero GUI/CLI, Ledger or Trezor through Feather or the GUI, Other), then paste or scan a receive address from it. X2F checks the address is a valid Monero address on the right network. The list shows each wallet's name and the end of its address, never a balance. **Adding a payout address requires confirming it's you** (a fresh PGP code, passkey, security key, or password plus any second factor), so a hijacked session cannot change it, and the app always shows an alert when one is added. New wallets may wait 24 hours depending on how the user confirms (6.3, "Wallet wait by method" and "Wallet rules refined").
- **Edge privacy tip.** Edge has long synced Monero through servers that saw users' view keys; it now offers Full Sync on the phone. The Add a wallet step for Edge says: "For best privacy, turn on Full Sync in Edge's Monero settings."
- **Funding.** "Open in <wallet>" uses a standard `monero:` payment link carrying the escrow address and exact amount. QR code and copy-address are the fallback, and the only option on desktop. **To verify in Phase 0:** Cake Wallet and Edge open `monero:` links and fill in the amount correctly on iOS and Android.
- **Payouts** go to the wallet the buyer picks when the trade starts; refunds go to the seller's saved wallet.
- **Confirm it's you for wallets** (owner, 2026-10-17). The first wallet, added during setup, skips the confirm step because the account was just created. Every wallet added later needs confirm-it's-you (the same proof as the login method), and an alert is always saved. Nobody can start a trade or post an ad until they have at least one payout wallet; the app sends them to Add a wallet first.
- **Address reuse.** Monero addresses never expire and can be paid any number of times; on chain each payment lands at a one-time stealth address, so reuse is not visible publicly. The counterparty does see the payout address, so the app offers an optional tip: "For extra privacy, paste a fresh address from your wallet for each trade." Exchange deposit addresses are the exception, since the exchange controls them.
- **What "connect" means here.** Monero has no MetaMask-style connect standard; WalletConnect in Cake and Edge covers other chains, not Monero, and no phone wallet can join a multisig. So "connecting" a wallet means saving its address and opening it to pay.
- **Per-trade escrow keys.** The escrow still needs a buyer key and a seller key, so the app creates **one temporary key per trade** on the device. It never holds a balance of its own, is encrypted on the device, and is deleted once the trade closes. It is backed up **encrypted to the user's own PGP key** so a lost phone mid-trade can be recovered on a new device; X2F cannot read it.
- **Future goal.** If a Monero wallet ever supports a connect standard with multisig signing, hand escrow signing to the user's own wallet and drop the in-app key.

### 6.5 Application security
- **Target standard:** OWASP ASVS Level 3.
- **Tamper defence.** The main risk for a web wallet is a compromised server sending tampered code. Defences:
  - a strict Content Security Policy,
  - no third-party scripts,
  - Subresource Integrity on every asset,
  - pinned and audited dependencies with a minimal dependency count,
  - signed, reproducible native builds,
  - large trades nudged toward the native app.
- **Encryption:**
  - trade chat is encrypted to the buyer, the seller and the trade's moderator key, so a moderator can read it once a dispute opens (6.6),
  - TLS everywhere,
  - the `.onion` site for Tor users.
- **Tooling.** Every change is scanned with Semgrep, Claude Security and Security Guidance; supply-chain checks and `npm audit` must be clean.
  - The feasibility test already flagged a high and a moderate advisory in monero-ts's transitive dependencies. These must be resolved before production.
- **Before launch:** an independent code and cryptography audit, a penetration test, then a public bug bounty.

## 8. Architecture

| Part | Choice | Why |
|---|---|---|
| App | TypeScript, React, Vite; installable PWA | One codebase, mature tooling |
| Native wrappers | Capacitor (iOS, Android) | Wraps the same web code; adds secure storage and Tor networking |
| Escrow keys | monero-ts (official Monero code compiled to WebAssembly) in the client | Per-trade escrow keys only, no user wallet; keys never leave the device; proven in the feasibility test |
| PGP | OpenPGP.js | Standard library with independent audits |
| Passkeys and security keys | WebAuthn | Built into browsers and phones |
| E2E encryption | libsodium | Mature and audited |
| Backend API | TypeScript (Fastify) + PostgreSQL | Ads, auth challenges, reputation, disputes; holds no user funds |
| Relay | WebSocket relay for chat and escrow messages | Passes only encrypted, signed messages |
| Monero nodes | X2F-run nodes (clearnet and .onion); users may set their own | Escrow sync and confirmations |
| Price feed | Median of several exchange sources | No single source can skew prices |

Escrow logic sits behind one interface (`EscrowEngine`), so the multisig implementation can change without touching the trade flow.

## 11. Open decisions

These need answers before or during the MVP. Each has a proposed default.

1. **Business model and fees. Decided 2026-10-14:** a flat **1%** fee on released trades and settled disputes (owner, 2026-10-29c), paid **only by the ad poster**, the person whose ad was taken, whether the ad is to buy or to sell (owner, 2026-10-29c: accepting a reply to your ad means accepting the 1% market fee); the person taking an ad pays no X2F fee. The ad poster learns of it at sign-up and in the terms of service, and sees it itemised on the released screen ("X2F fee (1%, your ad) −0.012500 XMR" above "You receive"); it is never shown to the person taking the ad. It is taken as a second output of the escrow release: added to the escrow when the ad poster is the seller, deducted from the payout when the ad poster is the buyer. Cancelled trades pay nothing (owner, 2026-10-29c): the buyer's cancel, a walk-away before funding, a mutual cancel, a no-show or lapse refund. **Settled disputes pay the 1%** (owner, 2026-10-29c; a settled dispute means X2F's service was used): when the buyer wins, as on a release; when the seller wins and the poster is the seller, the fee output comes from the escrow (the seller added it at funding). When the seller wins and the poster is the buyer, the escrow holds no fee: the poster still owes the 1% of the trade total, the ruling screen says so ("This also means you still owe the 1% fee for this trade, equal to <amount>, which will be deducted automatically from your future trades.", owner's meaning, final copy unsigned), and it is deducted automatically from their future trades (owner, 2026-10-29c). *Lead default, owner may change:* the amount owed is shown on the account and collected from the next settled trade(s) the account is part of until paid: added to that trade's fee output, so it is deducted from what the account receives, or added to what it funds as the seller. The owed amount is fixed in that trade's signed terms at the take, so both apps can check the fee output before signing. Cancels have no fee output and collect nothing. **Revenue ledger** (owner, 2026-10-29; 2026-10-29c): the system knows every released trade and settled dispute paid 1%, and never counts cancels and keeps a separate internal ledger of aggregates for revenue, profit, growth, payroll and treasury; no per-trade record is kept for it. **A second fee tier for large trades** comes later (threshold to be decided). Future revenue ideas: featured trader, trader banners, recommended trader per payment method. **Monero network fees** (decided 2026-10-17): the seller pays the funding transaction's fee from their own wallet; the release or refund transaction's fee comes out of the payout to whoever receives the XMR, never out of the X2F fee output. **Extra XMR sent to an escrow** (decided 2026-10-17, revised 2026-10-20 by the owner): if the escrow receives more than the trade amount before the buyer taps "I've paid", and the new total is still within the offer's minimum and maximum, the trade grows to the funded amount at the same locked rate. The app tells both sides the new total ("Trade grew to 1.05 XMR, you now pay $X"); the 1% fee is recalculated on the new amount and stays exact. If the buyer can't or doesn't want to pay the new total, he cancels and they redo the trade; the cancel refunds everything to the seller. If the new total would exceed the offer's maximum, or extra XMR arrives after the buyer has marked paid, the trade amount does not change and the extra goes back to the seller at the end, together with whatever the seller is owed. In the owner's words (2026-10-21): any extra goes back to the person who sent the XMR to begin with, which is the seller, the only party who funds the escrow (Monero hides senders, so X2F cannot tell them apart). This also holds once a dispute is open: the trade amount stays fixed and the extra returns to the seller at settlement. On any refund (buyer cancel, no-show, mutual cancel) everything goes to the seller. Overpaying should be rare, because funding uses a `monero:` link or QR code with the exact amount filled in. When extra XMR arrives late, the payout waits until every coin in the escrow has 10 confirmations (Monero's own unlock rule, about 20 minutes) and then pays everything; there is no separate time cap (owner, 2026-10-17). For comparison: LocalMonero charged 1% to the ad poster; Hodl Hodl 0.75% to each side; Peach 2% to the buyer; RoboSats 0.2% split.

6. **Price feed sources** and how to handle exchanges delisting XMR. **Decided 2026-10-24.**
   - **XMR price in US dollars.** The median of Kraken, Bitfinex and CoinGecko (lead's pick, approved), from at least 2 of them.
   - **Maximum gap between sources: 3%** (owner Q7, 6 Nov 2026; was 5% in the build, `maxSpreadBps` 500 -> 300). Beyond it X2F shows no price for that currency. Whether "3%" means "from the median" or "between any two sources" is awaiting the owner (review O1, 9 Nov). The **fourth source stays off by default** until it has passed a Tor check (owner Q6/F11); see "Before enabling the fourth price source" in docs/dev-setup.md.
   - **Every other currency** is that dollar price times the dollar exchange rate for the currency (owner: "list the price of the USD and then every currency against it"). So every currency gets a live market price, not only USD and EUR.
   - **Exchange rates: three sources in a fixed order of trust** (owner, 2026-10-24). X2F uses the most trusted source. The second is used only if the first is down or stale, and the third only if the first two are. This is not an average. The order is set in config by how much faith we have in each source's numbers.
   - **Rates must be fresh.** Sources must update at least hourly, and the server fetches every few minutes. A rate older than 1 hour counts as stale, and X2F moves to the next source. If no source has a fresh rate for a currency, market offers in that currency pause, just as when the XMR price is stale. The owner can change the 1-hour limit in Settings.
   - **Price health alerts** (owner, 2026-10-24). The server watches every price and rate source and tells the owner right away when something goes wrong, through the owner's in-app alerts and a phone push:
     - **1 failed fetch:** retry quietly.
     - **3 failures in a row, or a stale answer, from one source:** "Kraken price feed failing, using backup". The Price health panel shows which source is in use.
     - **Every source for a price or currency down:** an urgent alert, "USD market prices paused".
     - **A source recovered:** an all-clear alert.
     - Alerts are grouped, so a long outage sends one alert, not hundreds.
     - The owner dashboard's **Price health** panel lists each source with its last good fetch, its failure count and whether it is in use.
   - **The offer and trade screens show both:** the XMR price in the trade's currency, and its US-dollar value.
   - **No source swap needed.** If an exchange delists XMR, the server keeps working from the remaining sources. Sources are config, so swapping one is a settings change.
