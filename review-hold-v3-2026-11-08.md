## CRITICAL
1. A password thief can stop the real owner cancelling, so the thief's new password applies and the owner is locked out.
   holdRoutes.ts:115 (cancel), :77, :97; hold.ts:494; holdChanges.ts:46-47; throttle.ts:121-133.
   The 100/day cap is one bucket per account (`hold-changes:<accountId>`), shared by every session, and dailyCap counts every call before anything else, refused ones included. Cancel is behind it.
   Scenario: thief has the username and password (copied device cookie, or an approval the owner tapped). At 00:00:05 UTC he stages and submits a new password (due 00:00:05 next day), then sends 100 `DELETE /v1/account/hold/changes/<random uuid>` (each a 404, each counted). The owner sees the alert and taps Cancel all: 429 slow-down until midnight. A recovery-code login logs the thief out but its session's cancel hits the same 429 (G32 keeps the waiting change). The cap resets 5 seconds before the change applies, and the thief, if still logged in, burns the new day too. applyPassword (password.ts:378-381) then revokes every session but the thief's, the owner's recovery session included. The holdV3 test at :343 already shows a full bucket refusing DELETE /v1/account/hold/changes.
   Fix: never cap cancelling (it only takes power away; g65 "a cancelled change never applies"). Cap staging and submitting per session, count only successful ones, and coalesce the alerts instead. Test: a session at the cap still cancels; a second session's flood never blocks the owner.

2. Hold-paused trades resume within 5 seconds: the "frozen until a moderator decides" guarantee (G7, G33) never holds in production.
   packages/trade/src/timerLoop.ts:69; apps/api/src/main.ts:137-143 (startTimers runs every 5 s, TIMER_LOOP_MS); trades/held.ts:24-26 (the SEAM note says the loop "is not running in the API yet": wrong, it runs on main too).
   With maintenance off, decide() resumes every trade with paused_at set, whoever paused it. syncHeldTrades never pauses again (the row is already `paused`). The trade's deadlines then run, and /internal/v1/watch reports paused=false (internal/routes.ts:330), so the moderation service's timers (final alert, lapse escalation, the 6 h fallback) run too. Meanwhile refuseHeldTradeEvent still blocks the held party entirely and the partner's mark-paid.
   Scenario: buyer B has sent fiat; seller S's account goes into a recovery-code hold before B taps "I've paid". The trade is paused, resumed by the next timer pass, B's mark-paid is refused (409 counterparty-unavailable), the pay window and the moderator's timers run out, and the escrow is refunded to S with no moderator decision. B's only option is a dispute after the refund. A maintenance end resumes held trades the same way.
   Fix: the timer loop must not resume, and the watch must report paused, while the trade has an open held_trades row (filter in timers.ts's query, or pass the held codes into timerEvents); remove the SEAM note. Test with the real loop: a held trade stays paused across timer passes and across a maintenance on/off.

## IMPORTANT
3. A team member with the hold-cancel right can block their own removal or loss of rights for good.
   holdChanges.ts:198-204; holdRoutes.ts:130-140; routes/team.ts:191-213.

Scenario: the lead removes a rogue member; the member unticks it; this repeats every time. Only a strong login (which this lead may not have) or a recovery-code login gets the member out. The member keeps access the whole time (p5-7: "the member keeps their access until it applies").
   Fix: a member may not cancel or approve a waiting change that names them (team-remove, team-roles with memberId = their own). Ask the owner whether members may touch team changes at all (p4-8 grants the right; G57 "only a team lead can kick out team members").

4. A username-and-password owner can neither kick out a thief nor make their own password change land (owner g64).

And the owner's own submitted password change can be held back for ever: the thief submits any small change every ~23 h, which pushes the owner's due date back each time, at a cost of 2 actions.    Fix (owner/lead decision; it touches the banner's single date): give each change its own 24 h from its own submission, or never move a change already submitted; and let a weak session end weak sessions younger than itself, as R4-M1 already does for recovery sessions. ## Re-verify 561af62c (8 Nov). CHANGES REQUIRED: 1 new Critical (in the I5 fix); everything else closed.

### NEW CRITICAL (R-C1): the seller can forge the held buyer's cancel and get the escrow back
trades/held.ts refuseHeldTradeEvent, the dry run `apply(held.prev, { type: 'buyer-cancel' }, 'buyer', held.now)`; trades/routes.ts:212 (the `before` hook runs before the machine's own role check); held.ts syncHeldTrades carries out the recorded cancel as `'buyer'`.
The dry run hard-codes `'buyer'` as the actor. It never uses the caller's real side (`by`). When the BUYER's account is held, the partner is the seller. The seller sends `POST /v1/trades/:code/events {type:'buyer-cancel'}` and passes PARTNER_EXITS. The dry run as 'buyer' succeeds (secured, nothing paid, so settling), and the hook records `cancel_requested_at`, commits and returns 'stop'. So the machine's `by !== 'buyer'` check (machine.ts:577) never runs. When the hold ends, the sync applies `buyer-cancel` as 'buyer' and the escrow goes back to the seller. The buyer gave no consent and has no way to stop it: the held account is refused every event, dispute included, and the cancel runs straight after the hold's last row goes, before the buyer can tap "I've paid". This is exactly the C2 scenario (the buyer sent fiat, then their account went into a recovery-code hold before they tapped "I've paid"). The result is fiat lost and the coins returned, with no moderator.
Fix: pass the route's `by` and dry-run `apply(held.prev, e, by, now)`, or record only when `by === 'buyer'`. Let any other refusal fall through to the machine, so the seller gets 409 wrong-party. Test: buyer held, the seller posts buyer-cancel, the answer is 409 and `cancel_requested_at` stays null; after the hold ends the trade is still secured. (holdV3:888 covers only the case where the partner is the buyer.)

### C1: closed
Cancelling is never limited. Staging, submitting and approving are counted per session, inside the transaction, after the action succeeds (`dailyCapInTx`; a 429 rolls back the draft or submission). Callers of gateChange roll back on the throw. A thief can no longer use up the owner's cancel or Approve now (Approve now is the owner's strong session's own count). Alerts: a thief with many sessions can still write many submitted and cancelled alerts and push the owner's alert out of the 50-item list, but push still goes to every device. That is minor; coalescing is still advisable.
Residual, not in this diff but you asked: the recovery-code login can be locked by anyone. throttle.ts throttleKey uses one `recovery-code:<account>:anon` bucket per account (plus the device bucket, which a thief holding the copied cookie shares). After 10 wrong codes, only one try every 30 s is claimed by compare-and-set, and a script that fires exactly when each wait ends wins nearly every claim. The password quiet lock (15 min, re-lockable at once) works the same way. So a thief who holds the password and the copied cookie can keep a logged-out owner out of both the password login and the recovery login while his own change runs out its 24 h. Pre-existing; I recommend a follow-up task: let a correct recovery code through even when locked (16 characters cannot be guessed), or check the code before the lock refuses. That is the lead's call; it touches the owner's 23 Oct "quiet lock" rule only for recovery codes.

### C2: closed
timers.ts passes the set of trades with an open held_trades row. In timerLoop.ts decide(), with maintenance off, a held trade is never resumed or ticked. With maintenance on it is only paused, if it isn't paused already. An overdue escalation runs only for an unpaused lapsed trade, which a held trade practically never is, because pause fails only when the trade is already paused. /internal/v1/watch now reports `paused` when an open row exists. The SEAM note is gone. Other resume paths I checked: `resume` is applied only by the timer loop and by syncHeldTrades (grep). The staff hold-end and the minute job (syncHeldTradesJob) both go through syncHeldTrades, which resumes only once the trade's last row goes, under the trade lock (minor 10). Opening a dispute does not clear pausedAt (onDispute). A moderator's decision closes the trade, which is the owner's G7 exception. A maintenance off/on cycle leaves it paused. catchUpHeldTrades adds 'todo' rows, which pause.

### I3: closed
cancelChanges by id gives 403 when the change names the member, and Cancel all skips such changes (NAMES_MEMBER, $5). approveChanges gives 403 for any named id. Unticking is the same DELETE by id. The changes carry `memberId` (hold.ts:108/116, team.ts:194/209). The owner question about whether members may touch team changes at all is still open.

### I4: closed for the push-back; residuals stated
Each change keeps its own due_at, a submission no longer moves the others, and the banner and list show the next date. A thief can no longer push the owner's change back by submitting.
Remaining route: any session of the owner, a weak one included, may cancel any waiting change (g65 and p5-7 as built). So a thief can still cancel the owner's password change, and the owner must submit it again with a new 24 h. This is symmetric with the owner cancelling the thief's.
Weak may end newer weak (routes/account.ts): the target must be a full, non-member, waiting-strength session created strictly after the caller's, and members and recovery or strong targets are refused. Residual: when the thief's session is OLDER than every live session of the owner (the owner's phone session expired or was logged out before the theft), the thief can now end each new owner login. The sessions list shows createdAt, so a script can do this within a second, and it also ends the owner's push subscription. Before this change, neither side could. created_at is fixed at insert and the session's absolute lifetime limits it, so the thief cannot make his session look older. The recovery login covers this case: it logs everyone out, the thief cannot log in during the hold, cancelling works during the hold (no holdInTx in the cancel route) from the recovery session, which no weak session can end (level must be 'full'), and once the hold ends the waited recovery session's password change applies at once (gateChange `waited`), which revokes the rest. This holds only while the recovery login itself can't be blocked (see the C1 residual). Without a recovery code, a password-only owner against a password-only thief stays a stalemate unless the owner's live session is the older one. That is inherent in having one shared credential; tell the owner so.

### I5: the design is right, but see R-C1

After the hold, the trade is resumed and then the buyer-cancel is applied. A dispute opened meanwhile makes the machine refuse it (disputed is not in BUYER_CANCEL_SETTLE), and so does a moderator closing the trade. Double-apply: a rerun after a crash finds the trade in settling or closed and the machine refuses. When another hold still holds the trade, the record moves to its open rows under the trade lock. The `cancel_requested_at` column in 016 is fine because 016 is unreleased. Small point: the cancel is logged twice, `trade.buyer-cancel` when recorded and again when applied. Make the first `trade.buyer-cancel-recorded`.
